In 1859, 24 rabbits released in Australia multiplied into millions within a decade. Questions such as “when will the population reach a given size?” put the unknown in the exponent. Solving an exponential equation means finding where two exponential graphs meet.

Learning objectives Use like bases to solve exponential equations; use logarithms to solve exponential equations.

01 · Solving means finding an intersection

The solution of 2x + 1 = 22x − 1 is the x-coordinate where the two graphs cross. Trace x and look for the input where the two outputs agree.

INTERACTIVE · TRACE BOTH SIDES

02 · Equations with a common base

One-to-one property

For b > 0, b ≠ 1: bM = bN ⇔ M = N. If the bases differ, try to rewrite both sides as powers of the same base using (bm)n = bmn, 1/bm = b−m, and n√(bm) = bm/n. Then set the exponents equal and solve.

INTERACTIVE · REWRITE, MATCH EXPONENTS, SOLVE

Once both sides share a base, the equation is really about the exponents. The graph shows the two exponents as functions of x; they meet exactly at the solutions.

03 · Equations without a common base

Use logarithms

1. Take ln (or log) of both sides. 2. Use the power rule, ln(bM) = M·ln b, to bring the exponents down. 3. Solve the resulting linear equation: collect the x-terms, factor out x, and divide.

INTERACTIVE · apx + q = brx + s

After taking ln, each side is a straight line in x. Their intersection is the solution.
