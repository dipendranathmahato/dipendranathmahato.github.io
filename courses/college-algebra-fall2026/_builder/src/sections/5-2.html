Quadratics are polynomials of degree 2. Now we allow any whole-number power. The first question about any polynomial is what it does at the far ends of the graph, its end behavior, and the answer depends only on its leading term.

Learning objectives Identify power functions; identify the end behavior of power functions; identify polynomial functions; identify the degree and leading coefficient of a polynomial function; describe end behavior using arrow notation.

01 · Power functions f(x) = kxᵖ

Definition · Power function

A power function is a single term f(x) = kxᵖ: a real coefficient k times x raised to a fixed real power p. Examples: the area of a circle A(r) = πr², the volume of a sphere V(r) = (4/3)πr³, and ∛x = x^(1/3).

02 · Is it a power function?

Rewrite each function, if you can, as one term kxᵖ with a constant power p.

03 · Is it a polynomial?

Definition · Polynomial function

f(x) = aₙxⁿ + … + a₂x² + a₁x + a₀, where every power of x is a non-negative integer (0, 1, 2, …), the coefficients are real numbers, and aₙ ≠ 0. Simplify first: 2x³ · 3x + 4 = 6x⁴ + 4 is a polynomial.

04 · Degree, leading term, and leading coefficient

Vocabulary

Write the polynomial in descending powers. The degree is the highest power, the leading term is the term with that power, and the leading coefficient is its coefficient. A factored polynomial must be expanded (or its factors’ degrees added) first.

05 · End behavior: zoom out

Rule · The leading term decides the ends

Even degree: both ends point the same way, up if the leading coefficient is positive (x → ±∞, f(x) → ∞) and down if negative (f(x) → −∞). Odd degree: the ends point opposite ways: positive leading coefficient means down on the left and up on the right; negative means up on the left and down on the right.
