End behavior describes the far ends of a polynomial graph. This section fills in the middle: where the graph meets the x-axis, how it meets it (crossing, bouncing, or flattening), and how many times it can turn.

Learning objectives Recognize characteristics of graphs of polynomial functions; use factoring to find zeros; identify zeros and their multiplicities; determine end behavior; understand the relationship between degree and turning points; graph polynomial functions.

01 · Zeros and multiplicity

Rule · Behavior at a zero

If (x − h)ᵖ is a factor, then x = h is a zero of multiplicity p. Near h the graph looks like a power function: for p = 1 it crosses like a line; for even p it touches and bounces like a parabola; for odd p ≥ 3 it crosses while flattening like x³. The sum of the multiplicities is the degree, and a polynomial of degree n has at most n − 1 turning points.

02 · Factor to find the intercepts

Set f(x) = 0 and factor completely; each factor gives a zero. The y-intercept is f(0).

03 · From a graph to a formula

Each zero’s shape reveals its multiplicity: a crossing like a line (1), a bounce like a parabola (2), a flattened crossing like a cubic (3), or a flatter bounce (4). Choose the multiplicities, then the y-intercept fixes the leading coefficient a.

04 · Sketching a polynomial step by step

How to sketch

1. Find the intercepts. 2. Check for symmetry. 3. Use multiplicities for the behavior at each x-intercept. 4. Use the leading term for the end behavior. 5. Connect, with at most n − 1 turning points.
