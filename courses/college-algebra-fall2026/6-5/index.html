Where we are coming from

In 6.4 · Graphs of Logarithmic Functions, logarithmic graphs are reflections of exponential graphs over y = x.

Logarithms are exponents, so the rules of exponents turn into rules of logarithms: multiplying powers adds exponents, dividing subtracts them, and raising to a power multiplies them. These rules let us expand one complicated logarithm into simple pieces, or condense many pieces into one.

Learning objectives Use the product, quotient, and power rules for logarithms; expand logarithmic expressions; condense logarithmic expressions; use the change-of-base formula.

01 · Properties that come straight from the definition

For b > 0, b ≠ 1

b⁰ = 1 ⇔ logb1 = 0. b¹ = b ⇔ logbb = 1. Logarithms and exponentials undo each other: logb(bˣ) = x and blogbx = x for x > 0.

02 · Product, quotient, and power rules

Rules (b > 0, b ≠ 1, M > 0, N > 0)

Product: logb(MN) = logbM + logbN. Quotient: logb(M/N) = logbM − logbN. Power: logb(Mⁿ) = n·logbM.

INTERACTIVE · TEST A RULE WITH NUMBERS

Pick a rule and some numbers. Both sides always agree. Then try a common mistake and watch the two sides disagree.

03 · Expanding a logarithm

How to expand

Check the argument: 1. a power for the whole argument → power rule; 2. a quotient → quotient rule; 3. a product → product rule; 4. repeat on each new argument until none has a power, quotient, or product. Keep parentheses after a minus sign.

INTERACTIVE · ONE RULE PER STEP

04 · Condensing to a single logarithm

How to condense

1. Work inside parentheses first. 2. Move each coefficient up as a power (power rule). 3. From left to right, combine sums with the product rule. 4. From left to right, combine differences with the quotient rule.

INTERACTIVE · ONE RULE PER STEP

05 · The change-of-base formula

Change of base

For M > 0 and bases b, n (positive, not 1): logbM = lognM / lognb. With a calculator, use logbM = log M / log b = ln M / ln b.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · Logarithmic Properties

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Expand log₇(21x²(x − 8)).

    Show answer

    log₇(3 · 7 · x² · (x − 8)) = log₇3 + log₇7 + 2log₇x + log₇(x − 8) = log₇3 + 1 + 2log₇x + log₇(x − 8).

  2. Condense log₄5 + log₄2 + log₄(6x + 5) and log₇(8x + 1) − log₇(2x).

    Show answer

    log₄(10(6x + 5)) and log₇((8x + 1)/(2x)).

  3. Expand log₅(p⁴) and condense −4log(3x).

    Show answer

    4log₅p, and −4log(3x) = log((3x)⁻⁴) = log(1/(3x)⁴).

  4. Condense 4(ln(6x) + 3ln(x + 3) − ln(5x + 2)).

    Show answer

    Inside: ln(6x) + ln(x + 3)³ − ln(5x + 2) = ln(6x(x + 3)³/(5x + 2)). Then the power rule gives ln[(6x(x + 3)³/(5x + 2))⁴].
