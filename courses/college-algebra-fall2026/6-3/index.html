Where we are coming from

In 6.2 · Graphs of Exponential Functions, three key points and transformations draw any exponential graph.

An exponential function answers “what is b raised to this power?” A logarithm answers the reverse question: “what power of b gives this number?” Every logarithm statement is an exponential statement in disguise.

Learning objectives Convert from logarithmic to exponential form; convert from exponential to logarithmic form; evaluate common and natural logarithms.

01 · A logarithm is an exponent

Definition · Logarithm

For b > 0, b ≠ 1, and x > 0: y = logb x ⇔ by = x. The base stays the base, the logarithm is the exponent, and the argument is the result of the power.

03 · Evaluating logarithms

Common and natural logarithms

The common logarithm has base 10: log x means log10 x. The natural logarithm has base e: ln x means loge x. To evaluate a logarithm by hand, set it equal to x, convert to exponential form, and match exponents: log381 = x ⇒ 3ˣ = 81 = 3⁴ ⇒ x = 4.

04 · Logarithms on a calculator

Most logarithms are not whole numbers. A calculator gives log and ln directly; the result still means “the exponent that produces x.”

Check your understanding · Logarithmic Functions

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Write log₅(125) = 3 and log₄(1/64) = −3 in exponential form.

    Show answer

    5³ = 125 and 4⁻³ = 1/64.

  2. Write 10³ = 1,000 and 81/3 = 2 in logarithmic form.

    Show answer

    log(1,000) = 3 and log₈(2) = 1/3.

  3. Evaluate log(10,000) and log₅(625) without a calculator.

    Show answer

    10ˣ = 10,000 = 10⁴, so log(10,000) = 4. 5ˣ = 625 = 5⁴, so log₅(625) = 4.

  4. Why is log₂(−8) undefined?

    Show answer

    It asks for the power of 2 that gives −8, but 2y is positive for every y. The argument of a logarithm must be positive.
