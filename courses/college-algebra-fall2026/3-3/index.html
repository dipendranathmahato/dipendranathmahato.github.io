Where we are coming from

In 3.2 · Domain and Range, we described which inputs are allowed (domain) and which outputs occur (range).

A function can tell us more than single values. Comparing two outputs tells us how fast the function changes, and tracing a graph from left to right tells us where it rises, falls, or stays flat.

Learning objectives Find the average rate of change of a function from a formula, a table, and a graph; use a graph to determine where a function is increasing, decreasing, or constant.

01 · Average rate of change from a formula

Definition · Average rate of change

Over an interval [x₁, x₂], the average rate of change divides the change in outputs by the change in inputs:

Average rate of change = ΔyΔx = f(x₂) − f(x₁)x₂ − x₁

Geometrically, it is the slope of the secant line through the two points (x₁, f(x₁)) and (x₂, f(x₂)).

04 · Increasing, decreasing, or constant

Definitions

On an interval (a, b), a function is increasing if its outputs get larger as the inputs get larger, decreasing if its outputs get smaller as the inputs get larger, and constant if its outputs stay the same.

Always read the graph from left to right, and write the intervals using the x-values with parentheses.

INTERACTIVE · WALK THE GRAPH

Slide the walker from left to right. The walker reports whether the graph is going up, down, or staying flat at that spot. Write your intervals first, then turn on Show the intervals to check.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • Use x-values, not y-values, to describe where a function increases or decreases.
  • Read left to right. A graph that goes down as you move right is decreasing, even if it later rises.
  • Use parentheses at turning points: the function is neither increasing nor decreasing exactly at a turning point.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · Rates of Change and Behavior

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. What does the average rate of change of f on [x₁, x₂] measure, and how do you compute it?

    Show answer

    It measures how much the output changes, on average, per unit change in the input. Compute (f(x₂) − f(x₁)) / (x₂ − x₁); it is the slope of the secant line through the two points.

  2. Find the average rate of change of f(x) = x² − 3x on the interval [−1, 2].

    Show answer

    f(−1) = 1 + 3 = 4 and f(2) = 4 − 6 = −2. The rate is (−2 − 4)/(2 − (−1)) = −6/3 = −2.

  3. The table shows x = 0, 2, 5 with outputs 4, 10, 1. What is the average rate of change from x = 0 to x = 5?

    Show answer

    (1 − 4)/(5 − 0) = −3/5. On average the output drops 3/5 of a unit per unit of x.

  4. How do you find the intervals where a function is increasing from its graph?

    Show answer

    Trace the graph from left to right. Where it rises, record the x-interval with parentheses, for example (−∞, −2) ∪ (2, ∞).

  5. Can the average rate of change be 0 on an interval even if the function is not constant there?

    Show answer

    Yes. For g(x) = (x − 1)², g(0) = g(2) = 1, so the rate on [0, 2] is 0, but the graph goes down and then up in between.
