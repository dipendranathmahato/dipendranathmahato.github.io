Divide one polynomial by another and you get a rational function. The division brings something polynomials never had: inputs that are not allowed. Near those inputs the graph can have a hole or shoot off to infinity along a vertical asymptote, and far away it can level off along a horizontal asymptote.

Learning objectives Use arrow notation; find the domains of rational functions; identify vertical asymptotes; identify horizontal asymptotes; graph rational functions.

01 · Rational functions and their domains

Definition · Rational function

A rational function can be written as f(x) = p(x)/q(x), where p and q are polynomials and q(x) ≠ 0. As in Section 3.2, the denominator cannot be 0: the domain is all real numbers except the zeros of the denominator. Find the domain before canceling any common factors.

02 · Holes: removable discontinuities

Definition · Removable discontinuity

If x − a is a factor of both the numerator and the denominator, with multiplicity in the numerator at least as large as in the denominator, the graph has a hole at x = a. Factor both parts, cancel the common factor, and evaluate the reduced function at a to find the height of the hole.

03 · Arrow notation

Reading the arrows

x → a⁻: x approaches a from the left (x < a). x → a⁺: x approaches a from the right (x > a). x → ∞ or x → −∞: x grows without bound in that direction. f(x) → ∞, f(x) → −∞, or f(x) → c: the outputs grow without bound or approach c.

05 · Horizontal and slant asymptotes

Compare the degrees of the numerator (n) and the denominator (d)

Case 1, n < d: the horizontal asymptote is y = 0. Case 2, n = d: the horizontal asymptote is y = (leading coefficient of the numerator)/(leading coefficient of the denominator). Case 3, n > d: no horizontal asymptote; when n = d + 1 there is a slant asymptote, the quotient from dividing the numerator by the denominator.

06 · Graphing a rational function in 8 steps

How to graph a rational function

1. Factor. 2. Find the domain. 3. Reduce by canceling common factors. 4. Mark the holes. 5. Find the x- and y-intercepts (multiplicity: odd crosses, even bounces). 6. Find the vertical asymptotes and the behavior near each. 7. Find the horizontal or slant asymptote. 8. Sketch.
