Bacteria colonies, viruses, radioactive isotopes, and medicines in the body all change at a rate proportional to how much is present. One formula models them all, and the equation-solving tools of Section 6.6 answer the questions: how much, and when?

Learning objective Model exponential growth and decay.

01 · The model A = A₀ekt

Exponential growth and decay

A(t) = A₀ekt, where A₀ is the starting amount, t is time, and k is the rate: k > 0 for growth, k < 0 for decay. The doubling time is the time to reach 2A₀; the half-life is the time to fall to A₀/2.

INTERACTIVE · GROWTH, DECAY, AND THE CONSTANT DOUBLING TIME

Notice that the doubling time (or half-life) does not depend on how much you start with or when you start counting: every equal time step multiplies the amount by the same factor.

02 · Finding k from a doubling time or half-life

Formulas

Doubling: 2A₀ = A₀ekt ⇒ 2 = ekt ⇒ ln 2 = kt ⇒ k = ln 2 / t, and the doubling time is t = ln 2 / k.

Half-life: ½A₀ = A₀ekt ⇒ ln(½) = kt ⇒ k = −ln 2 / t, and the half-life is t = −ln 2 / k.

The rate k belongs to the substance or population: once you find it, it stays the same even when A₀, A, and t change.
