Chapter 3

Functions

7 sections · Weeks 1–4

SECTION 3.1Week 1Functions and Function NotationRelations, function notation, and reading inputs and outputs from formulas, tables, and graphs. What makes a rule a function, and how do we read f(x)?SECTION 3.2Week 2Domain and RangeInterval and set-builder notation, domains from formulas, and domain and range from graphs. Which inputs are allowed, and which outputs can actually happen?SECTION 3.3Week 2Rates of Change and Behavior of GraphsAverage rate of change from formulas, tables, and graphs; increasing, decreasing, and constant intervals. How fast is a function changing, and where is it rising or falling?SECTION 3.4Week 3Composition of FunctionsFunction arithmetic, composite functions from formulas, tables, and graphs, and their domains. What happens when the output of one function becomes the input of another?SECTION 3.5 PART IWeek 3Transformations: ShiftsVertical and horizontal shifts of toolkit graphs, tables, and combined shifts. How can one small change to a formula slide a whole graph?SECTION 3.5 PART IIWeek 4Transformations: Reflections, Stretches, and CompressionsReflections over the axes, vertical and horizontal stretches and compressions, and combinations. How do we flip, stretch, and squeeze a graph, and in what order?SECTION 3.7Week 4Inverse FunctionsOne-to-one functions, the horizontal line test, finding and verifying inverses, and restricting domains. Can a function be run backward?

Systems of Equations

1 section · Week 5

SECTION 7.1Week 5Systems of Linear Equations: Two VariablesSolving systems by graphing, substitution, and elimination; independent, inconsistent, and dependent systems. Where do two conditions hold at the same time?
Chapter 5

Polynomial and Rational Functions

5 sections · Weeks 6–8

SECTION 5.1Week 6Quadratic FunctionsVertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, general and standard form, and maximum or minimum values. Where does a parabola turn, and how high or low can it go?SECTION 5.2Week 7Power Functions and Polynomial FunctionsPower functions, polynomial vocabulary (degree, leading term), and end behavior. What does a polynomial do far to the left and far to the right?SECTION 5.3Week 7Graphs of Polynomial FunctionsIntercepts by factoring, zeros and multiplicity, turning points, and sketching polynomial graphs. What happens in the middle of a polynomial graph?SECTION 5.5Week 8Zeros of Polynomial FunctionsThe Division Algorithm, Remainder Theorem, Factor Theorem, and synthetic division. How do we find zeros when factoring is not obvious?SECTION 5.6Week 8Rational FunctionsDomains, holes, intercepts, arrow notation, and vertical, horizontal, and slant asymptotes. What happens when we divide one polynomial by another?
Chapter 6

Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

7 sections · Weeks 10–13

SECTION 6.1Week 10Exponential FunctionsExponential functions f(x) = abˣ, the number e, and compound interest. What kind of function models growth by a percentage?SECTION 6.2Week 10Graphs of Exponential FunctionsKey points, asymptotes, and transformations of exponential graphs. How do three key points and transformations draw any exponential graph?SECTION 6.3Week 11Logarithmic FunctionsLogarithms as exponents: converting between forms, common and natural logarithms. If bʸ = x, how do we solve for the exponent y?SECTION 6.4Week 11Graphs of Logarithmic FunctionsDomains of logarithmic functions, key points, vertical asymptotes, and transformations. What does the inverse of an exponential look like?SECTION 6.5Week 12Logarithmic PropertiesProduct, quotient, and power rules; expanding and condensing; change of base. Which rules let us expand, condense, and evaluate logarithms?SECTION 6.6Week 12Exponential EquationsCommon-base equations, solving with logarithms, and reading solutions as intersections. How do we solve an equation when x is in the exponent?SECTION 6.7Week 13Exponential and Logarithmic ModelsExponential growth and decay, doubling time, half-life, and radiocarbon dating. How long until a quantity doubles or halves?

Learning objectives

Learn to simplify expressions; solve equations, inequalities, and systems; identify functions, domains, and ranges; use transformations and inverse functions; and graph polynomial, rational, exponential, and logarithmic functions.
