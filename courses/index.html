Chapter 1

Functions and Graphs

5 sections · Weeks 1–2

SECTION 1.1Week 1Review of FunctionsFunction notation, domain and range, interval notation, graphs, the vertical line test, piecewise functions, and composition. What makes a rule a function, and how do we read one from a formula, a table, or a graph?SECTION 1.2Week 1Basic Classes of FunctionsLinear functions and slope, polynomials, power and rational functions, algebraic and transcendental functions, and shifts, stretches, and reflections. Which families of functions will we meet again and again, and how do transformations move them?SECTION 1.3Week 2Trigonometric FunctionsRadians, the unit circle, the six trigonometric functions, identities, and the graphs of sine, cosine, and tangent. How do angles on the unit circle turn into the six trigonometric functions?SECTION 1.4Week 2Inverse FunctionsOne-to-one functions, the horizontal line test, finding inverses, reflection across y = x, restricted domains, and inverse trigonometric functions. When can we undo a function, and what does the graph of the inverse look like?SECTION 1.5Week 2Exponential and Logarithmic FunctionsExponential functions and their laws, the number e, logarithms as inverses, properties of logarithms, and solving exponential equations. What kind of growth multiplies instead of adds, and how do logarithms undo it?
Chapter 2

Limits

4 sections · Weeks 3–4

SECTION 2.1Week 3A Preview of CalculusThe tangent problem (secant slopes approaching a tangent), instantaneous velocity, and the area problem (rectangles under a curve). How can a sequence of approximations lead to an exact slope or an exact area?SECTION 2.2Week 3The Limit of a FunctionLimits from tables and graphs, one-sided limits, when a limit does not exist, infinite limits, and vertical asymptotes. What value does f(x) approach as x gets close to a, even if f(a) is undefined?SECTION 2.3Week 4The Limit LawsLimit laws, direct substitution, 0/0 forms handled by factoring, rationalizing, and simplifying, and the squeeze theorem. How can we compute limits exactly instead of guessing from tables?SECTION 2.4Week 4ContinuityContinuity at a point, removable, jump, and infinite discontinuities, continuity on intervals, and the Intermediate Value Theorem. What does it mean to draw a graph without lifting the pencil?
Chapter 3

Derivatives

7 sections · Weeks 5–8

SECTION 3.1Week 5Defining the DerivativeThe difference quotient, the derivative at a point as a limit, tangent lines, and the derivative as an instantaneous rate of change. How do secant slopes become the slope of the tangent line?SECTION 3.2Week 5The Derivative as a Functionf′(x) from the limit definition, graphing f′ from f, where a function fails to be differentiable, and higher derivatives. If every point has a slope, what does the slope function look like?SECTION 3.3Week 5Differentiation RulesThe constant, power, sum, and constant-multiple rules, the product and quotient rules, and combining the rules. How can we find derivatives without computing a limit every time?SECTION 3.5Week 6Derivatives of Trigonometric FunctionsThe derivatives of sin x and cos x, the other four trigonometric functions, and higher derivatives of sine and cosine. Why is the slope of sin x given by cos x?SECTION 3.6Week 6The Chain RuleThe chain rule for compositions, inside and outside functions, powers and trigonometric functions, chain rule with tables of values, and the derivatives of the inverse trigonometric functions. How do we differentiate a function inside a function?SECTION 3.9Week 7Derivatives of Exponential and Logarithmic FunctionsDerivatives of eˣ and bˣ, of ln x and logb x, the chain rule with exponentials and logarithms, and logarithmic differentiation. Which function is its own derivative, and what is the derivative of ln x?SECTION 3.4Week 8Derivatives as Rates of ChangeAmount of change and average rate, position, velocity, speed, and acceleration, speeding up and slowing down, and marginal cost. What does the derivative tell us about motion and other changing quantities?
Chapter 4

Applications of Derivatives

7 sections · Weeks 8–11

SECTION 4.2Week 8Linear Approximations and DifferentialsThe linear approximation L(x), estimating roots and powers, differentials dy and dx, and propagated error. How can a tangent line estimate values that are hard to compute?SECTION 4.3Week 9Maxima and MinimaAbsolute and local extrema, the Extreme Value Theorem, critical points, Fermat's theorem, and the closed interval method. Where can a function reach its highest or lowest value?SECTION 4.5Week 9Derivatives and the Shape of a GraphIncreasing and decreasing intervals, the first derivative test, concavity, inflection points, and the second derivative test. What do f′ and f″ reveal about where a graph rises, falls, and bends?SECTION 4.7Week 10Applied Optimization ProblemsSetting up an objective function and a constraint, the feasible interval, and maximizing area and volume or minimizing cost and distance. How do we find the best design when a quantity depends on a choice we make?SECTION 4.6Week 10Limits at Infinity and AsymptotesLimits at infinity, horizontal asymptotes, end behavior of polynomials and rational functions, and oblique asymptotes. What happens to f(x) as x grows without bound?SECTION 4.8Week 10L’Hôpital’s RuleL’Hôpital’s rule, rewriting 0 · ∞ and ∞ − ∞, indeterminate powers, and growth rates of functions. How can derivatives settle limits of the form 0/0 and ∞/∞?SECTION 4.10Week 11AntiderivativesAntiderivatives and the + C, indefinite integrals, basic integration formulas, and initial-value problems. Can we run differentiation backwards?
Chapter 5

Integration

7 sections · Weeks 11–14

SECTION 5.1Week 11Approximating AreasSigma notation and sum formulas, left, right, and midpoint approximations, and the exact area as a limit of sums. How can rectangles approximate the area under a curve?SECTION 5.2Week 11The Definite IntegralThe definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums, net signed area, properties of the integral, and the average value of a function. What exact number do the Riemann sums approach?SECTION 5.3Week 12The Fundamental Theorem of CalculusThe accumulation function, FTC Part 1 (with the chain rule), evaluating definite integrals with FTC Part 2, and the Mean Value Theorem for integrals. Why does an antiderivative compute an area?SECTION 5.4Week 13Integration Formulas and the Net Change TheoremBasic integration formulas, the net change theorem, displacement versus total distance, and integrals of even and odd functions. How does integrating a rate give the total change?SECTION 5.5Week 13Substitutionu-substitution for indefinite integrals, choosing u, and substitution for definite integrals with changed limits. How do we undo the chain rule?SECTION 5.6Week 13Integrals Involving Exponential and Logarithmic FunctionsIntegrals of eˣ and bˣ, the integral of 1/x = ln|x|, the integral of ln x, substitution with exponentials and logarithms, and a price-demand application. Which integral produces a logarithm?SECTION 5.7Week 14Integrals Resulting in Inverse Trigonometric FunctionsIntegrals of 1/√(a² − u²), 1/(a² + u²), and 1/(u√(u² − a²)), and substitution into the inverse trigonometric forms. Which integrals give inverse trigonometric functions?

CHAPTER 0 COURSE FOUNDATIONS

Introduction to Calculus I

Calculus studies two key problems: how to find an instantaneous rate of change, and how to find a total accumulation (the area under a curve). Both are solved with what you know from high-school mathematics plus one new idea, the limit. This is a guide to the expectations, grading, and exams for MATH 1210-12 in Fall 2024.

Learning outcomes

Analyze functions given graphically, numerically, analytically, or verbally; compute limits; interpret derivatives and integrals as limiting processes; use the derivative as a rate of change and a local linear approximation; interpret the definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums and as the net accumulation of a rate; relate derivatives and integrals through the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus; model physical situations with functions, derivatives, and integrals; and communicate solutions in clear, well-organized sentences.
