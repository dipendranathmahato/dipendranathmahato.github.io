A function can tell us more than single values. Comparing two outputs tells us how fast the function changes, and tracing a graph from left to right tells us where it rises, falls, or stays flat.

Learning objectives Find the average rate of change of a function from a formula, a table, and a graph; use a graph to determine where a function is increasing, decreasing, or constant.

01 · Average rate of change from a formula

Definition · Average rate of change

Over an interval [x₁, x₂], the average rate of change divides the change in outputs by the change in inputs:

Average rate of change = ΔyΔx = f(x₂) − f(x₁)x₂ − x₁

Geometrically, it is the slope of the secant line through the two points (x₁, f(x₁)) and (x₂, f(x₂)).

04 · Increasing, decreasing, or constant

Definitions

On an interval (a, b), a function is increasing if its outputs get larger as the inputs get larger, decreasing if its outputs get smaller as the inputs get larger, and constant if its outputs stay the same.

Always read the graph from left to right, and write the intervals using the x-values with parentheses.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • Use x-values, not y-values, to describe where a function increases or decreases.
  • Read left to right. A graph that goes down as you move right is decreasing, even if it later rises.
  • Use parentheses at turning points: the function is neither increasing nor decreasing exactly at a turning point.
