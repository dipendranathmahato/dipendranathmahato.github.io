Where we are coming from

In 6.1 · Exponential Functions, exponential functions abˣ model growth by a percentage, as in compound interest.

Three points are enough to sketch y = bˣ. Every other exponential graph is that sketch moved by the transformations of Section 3.5, so the plan is: plot three key points, then move them.

Learning objectives Graph exponential functions; graph exponential functions using transformations.

01 · Three key points of y = bˣ

Characteristics of f(x) = bˣ

Key points (−1, 1/b), (0, 1) (the y-intercept), and (1, b). Domain (−∞, ∞), range (0, ∞), horizontal asymptote y = 0, no x-intercept. The graph is one-to-one: it passes the horizontal line test. It increases if b > 1 and decreases if 0 < b < 1.

02 · Transforming y = bˣ

Transformations of y = a·bx − h + k

|a| > 1: vertical stretch by |a|. 0 < |a| < 1: vertical compression. a < 0: reflection over the x-axis. b−x: reflection over the y-axis. h > 0 shifts right, h < 0 shifts left. k > 0 shifts up, k < 0 shifts down. Only the vertical shift moves the asymptote: it becomes y = k.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · Graphs of Exponential Functions

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. What are the three key points of y = (1/2)ˣ?

    Show answer

    (−1, 1/b) = (−1, 2), (0, 1), and (1, b) = (1, 1/2). The graph decreases toward the asymptote y = 0.

  2. Graph y = −2(4)x + 3 + 1 from the key points of y = 4ˣ.

    Show answer

    Multiply the y-coordinates by −2, shift 3 left, then shift 1 up: (−1, 1/4) → (−4, 1/2), (0, 1) → (−3, −1), (1, 4) → (−2, −7). Asymptote y = 1; y-intercept (0, −127); range (−∞, 1).

  3. Graph y = −3(2/5)x − 2 − 4 from the key points of y = (2/5)ˣ.

    Show answer

    (−1, 5/2) → (1, −23/2), (0, 1) → (2, −7), (1, 2/5) → (3, −26/5). Asymptote y = −4; range (−∞, −4); y-intercept −3(2/5)⁻² − 4 = −3(25/4) − 4 = −91/4.
