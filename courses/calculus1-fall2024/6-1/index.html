Where we are coming from

In 5.7 · Integrals Resulting in Inverse Trigonometric Functions, we found integrals that produce inverse trigonometric functions.

The definite integral gives the area between a curve and the x-axis. The same idea gives the area between two curves: slice the region into thin rectangles whose height is the distance between the curves, add, and take the limit. Depending on the shape, it is easier to slice vertically (integrate in x) or horizontally (integrate in y).

Learning objectives Find the area between two curves as ∫ (upper − lower) dx; find the limits of integration from the points of intersection; split the integral where the curves cross; integrate with respect to y as ∫ (right − left) dy when that is simpler.

01 · Area between curves: slicing vertically

Upper minus lower

If f and g are continuous and f(x) ≥ g(x) on [a, b], the area of the region between y = f(x) (upper), y = g(x) (lower), x = a, and x = b is

A = ∫ab [f(x) − g(x)] dx.

Each vertical rectangle has height f(xᵢ*) − g(xᵢ*) and width Δx; this is a Riemann sum. For an enclosed region no interval is given: the limits a and b are the x-coordinates of the points where the curves meet.

Example: y = x + 4 and y = x² − 2x. Setting x + 4 = x² − 2x gives x² − 3x − 4 = (x + 1)(x − 4) = 0, so x = −1 and x = 4, and the line is on top between them: A = ∫−14 [(x + 4) − (x² − 2x)] dx = ∫−14 (−x² + 3x + 4) dx = 125/6.

02 · When the curves cross

Split at the crossings

If the curves cross inside [a, b], the upper curve changes. The area is ∫ab |f(x) − g(x)| dx: split the interval at each crossing and use (upper − lower) on each piece. For y = cos x and y = sin x on [0, π]: they cross at x = π/4, cos x is on top on [0, π/4], and sin x is on top on [π/4, π], so A = ∫0π/4 (cos x − sin x) dx + ∫π/4π (sin x − cos x) dx = (√2 − 1) + (1 + √2) = 2√2.

03 · Slicing horizontally: integrating in y

Right minus left

If the region is bounded by x = f(y) on the right and x = g(y) on the left, with f(y) ≥ g(y) for c ≤ y ≤ d, then

A = ∫cd [f(y) − g(y)] dy.

Example: the region enclosed by x = y²/2 − 3 and y = x − 1. Write the line as x = y + 1; then y²/2 − 3 = y + 1 gives (y − 4)(y + 2) = 0, so the curves meet at (5, 4) and (−1, −2). Slicing horizontally: A = ∫−24 [(y + 1) − (y²/2 − 3)] dy = 18. Slicing vertically also works, but the lower boundary changes at x = −1, so it takes two integrals and a substitution.

  1. Find the area enclosed by y = x + 4 and y = x² − 2x.

    Show answer

    They meet at x = −1 and x = 4, and the line is on top. A = ∫−14 (−x² + 3x + 4) dx = [−x³/3 + 3x²/2 + 4x]−14 = 56/3 − (−13/6) = 125/6.

  2. Find the area between y = cos x and y = sin x on [0, π].

    Show answer

    Split at π/4: ∫0π/4 (cos x − sin x) dx + ∫π/4π (sin x − cos x) dx = (√2 − 1) + (1 + √2) = 2√2.

  3. Find the area enclosed by x = y²/2 − 3 and y = x − 1.

    Show answer

    In y: ∫−24 [(y + 1) − (y²/2 − 3)] dy = [−y³/6 + y²/2 + 4y]−24 = 40/3 − (−14/3) = 18.
