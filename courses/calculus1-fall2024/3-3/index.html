Where we are coming from

In 3.2 · The Derivative as a Function, we built the derivative function f′ and read its graph from the graph of f.

Computing every derivative from the limit definition is slow. A handful of rules, each proved once from the definition, lets us differentiate any polynomial, power, product, or quotient directly. Often the most important step is rewriting the function first: roots as fractional powers, and quotients by a single power of x as sums.

Learning objectives Use the constant, power, sum and difference, and constant-multiple rules; rewrite roots and reciprocals as powers before differentiating; use the product rule and understand where it comes from; use the quotient rule; combine the rules and check an answer.

01 · The basic rules

Rules
constant and identityd/dx (c) = 0, d/dx (x) = 1
power ruled/dx (xⁿ) = n xⁿ⁻¹ (any real n)
sum and differenced/dx (f ± g) = f′ ± g′
constant multipled/dx (c·f) = c·f′

The power rule works for every real exponent, so rewrite first: ⁿ√(xᵐ) = xm/n and 1/xⁿ = x⁻ⁿ.

INTERACTIVE · THE POWER RULE FOR ANY EXPONENT

02 · Rewrite, then differentiate

Two habits

Roots become powers: 5√(x³) − 2∛(x¹¹) = 5x3/2 − 2x11/3. Divide out a single power of x: (x³ + 4)(x² + 1)/x = (x⁵ + x³ + 4x² + 4)/x = x⁴ + x² + 4x + 4x⁻¹. Then the power rule does the rest.

03 · The product rule

Product rule

d/dx [f(x)g(x)] = f′(x)g(x) + f(x)g′(x)

Proof idea: with A(x) = f(x)g(x), add and subtract f(x)g(x + h) in the numerator: A(x + h) − A(x) = [f(x + h) − f(x)]g(x + h) + f(x)[g(x + h) − g(x)]. Divide by h and let h → 0. The derivative of a product is not the product of the derivatives.

INTERACTIVE · A GROWING RECTANGLE

Think of f(x)g(x) as the area of a rectangle with width f(x) and height g(x). When x grows by h, the area grows by two strips and a tiny corner.

INTERACTIVE · PRODUCT RULE EXAMPLES

04 · The quotient rule

Quotient rule

d/dx [f(x)/g(x)] = (g(x)f′(x) − f(x)g′(x))/[g(x)]², where g(x) ≠ 0.

A way to remember it: “low d-high minus high d-low, over low low.” The order in the numerator matters because of the minus sign.

Check your understanding · Differentiation Rules

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Differentiate h(x) = 5√(x³) − 2∛(x¹¹).

    Show answer

    h(x) = 5x3/2 − 2x11/3, so h′(x) = (15/2)x1/2 − (22/3)x8/3.

  2. Differentiate g(x) = (x³ + 4)(x² + 1)/x.

    Show answer

    g(x) = x⁴ + x² + 4x + 4x⁻¹, so g′(x) = 4x³ + 2x + 4 − 4/x².

  3. Differentiate (x² + 1)/(x − 3) with the quotient rule.

    Show answer

    ((x − 3)(2x) − (x² + 1)(1))/(x − 3)² = (x² − 6x − 1)/(x − 3)².
