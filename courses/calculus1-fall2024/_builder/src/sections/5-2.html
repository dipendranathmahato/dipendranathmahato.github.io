In Section 5.1 the limit of rectangle sums gave the area under a positive function. The same limit makes sense for any continuous function, positive or not; it is called the definite integral. Where the graph is below the x-axis the rectangles have negative heights, so the integral measures net signed area: area above the axis counts as positive and area below counts as negative.

Learning objectives Define the definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums and use its notation; interpret it as net signed area; evaluate integrals from geometry; distinguish net signed area from total area; use the properties of the definite integral; find the average value of a function.

01 · The definite integral as net signed area

Definition

If f is defined on [a, b], the definite integral of f from a to b is

∫ab f(x) dx = limn→∞ Σi=1n f(xᵢ*) Δx,

provided the limit exists and is the same for every choice of sample points xᵢ* (then f is integrable; every continuous function is). In particular ∫ab f(x) dx = lim Lₙ = lim Rₙ. Here f is the integrand, a and b are the limits of integration, and x is a dummy variable: ∫ab f(x) dx = ∫ab f(t) dt.

A definite integral is a number (a net signed area); an indefinite integral ∫ f(x) dx is a family of functions (the antiderivatives).

02 · Integrals from geometry

Use known areas

When the region is made of triangles, rectangles, and pieces of circles, the integral can be found without any limit. In the graph below, f on [−4, 0] is the lower half of the circle of radius 2 centered at (−2, 0), and on [0, 4] it is a triangle with base 4 and height 2. The semicircle has area ½π(2²) = 2π and lies below the axis; the triangle has area ½(4)(2) = 4. So ∫−44 f(x) dx = −2π + 4, while the total area is 2π + 4.

03 · Properties of the definite integral

Properties
zero width∫aa f(x) dx = 0
reversing the limits∫ba f(x) dx = −∫ab f(x) dx
additivity∫ab f dx = ∫ac f dx + ∫cb f dx
linearity∫ (f ± g) = ∫ f ± ∫ g and ∫ c f = c ∫ f

Comparison: if f(x) ≥ g(x) on [a, b], then ∫ab f(x) dx ≥ ∫ab g(x) dx; if m ≤ f(x) ≤ M on [a, b], then m(b − a) ≤ ∫ab f(x) dx ≤ M(b − a).

04 · The average value of a function

Average value

The average of n numbers is their sum divided by n. Averaging f at many evenly spaced points and letting n → ∞ gives

fave = (1/(b − a)) ∫ab f(x) dx.

Geometrically, the rectangle of height fave over [a, b] has the same net area as the region under f.
