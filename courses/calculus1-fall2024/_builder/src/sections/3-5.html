Look at the graph of sin x and track the slope of its tangent lines: slope 1 at x = 0, slope 0 at the top (x = π/2), slope −1 at x = π, slope 0 at the bottom. These slopes trace out the graph of cos x. From d/dx sin x = cos x and d/dx cos x = −sin x, the quotient rule gives the derivatives of the other four trigonometric functions.

Learning objectives Explain why the derivative of sin x is cos x; state the derivatives of all six trigonometric functions; find tangent lines to trigonometric graphs; combine the trigonometric derivatives with the product and quotient rules; compute higher derivatives of sine and cosine.

01 · The slope of sin x is cos x

Sine and cosine

d/dx (sin x) = cos x and d/dx (cos x) = −sin x.

The proof uses the limit definition, the sum formula sin(x + h) = sin x cos h + cos x sin h, and the two limits limh→0 (sin h)/h = 1 and limh→0 (cos h − 1)/h = 0. (Angles are in radians; with degrees these formulas would need an extra factor.)

02 · Derivatives of the six functions

Formulas
sine and cosine(sin x)′ = cos x
(cos x)′ = −sin x
tangent and cotangent(tan x)′ = sec²x
(cot x)′ = −csc²x
secant and cosecant(sec x)′ = sec x tan x
(csc x)′ = −csc x cot x
a patternthe three “co” functions (cos, cot, csc) have derivatives with a minus sign

For example, tan x = sin x/cos x, so by the quotient rule (tan x)′ = (cos x · cos x − sin x · (−sin x))/cos²x = (cos²x + sin²x)/cos²x = 1/cos²x = sec²x.

03 · Tangent lines to trigonometric graphs

Example

For f(x) = 6 sin x at x = π/6: the point is (π/6, 6 · ½) = (π/6, 3); the slope is f′(π/6) = 6 cos(π/6) = 6 · √3/2 = 3√3. So y − 3 = 3√3(x − π/6), that is, y = 3√3 x + 3 − √3π/2.

05 · Higher derivatives of sine and cosine

A cycle of four

sin x → cos x → −sin x → −cos x → sin x → … The derivatives repeat every four steps, so the n-th derivative depends only on the remainder of n divided by 4.
