For f(x) = aˣ the difference quotient factors as aˣ · (aʰ − 1)/h, and the second factor does not depend on x. So the derivative of an exponential function is a constant multiple of the function itself, and the constant is ln a. For a = e the constant is 1: eˣ is its own derivative. Because logarithms are inverses of exponentials, their derivatives follow from the inverse function rule.

Exponential functions

For a > 0: (aˣ)′ = limh→0 (ax + h − aˣ)/h = aˣ · limh→0 (aʰ − 1)/h = aˣ ln a. In particular (eˣ)′ = eˣ and (aˣ)′ = aˣ ln a. With the chain rule: (eg(x))′ = eg(x) g′(x) and (ag(x))′ = ag(x) ln a · g′(x).

03 · Derivatives of logarithmic functions

Logarithms

Differentiate alogₐx = x with the chain rule: alogₐx ln a · (logₐx)′ = 1, that is, x ln a · (logₐx)′ = 1. So

(ln x)′ = 1/x and (logₐx)′ = 1/(x ln a) for x > 0.

Also (ln|x|)′ = 1/x for x ≠ 0, and by the chain rule (ln g(x))′ = g′(x)/g(x). The domain of a logarithm is always x > 0 (or g(x) > 0).

Before differentiating

05 · Logarithmic differentiation

Method

For y = f(x)g(x), or a long product and quotient: (1) take ln of both sides; (2) expand with log properties; (3) differentiate implicitly: y′/y = …; (4) multiply by y. Neither the power rule (constant exponent) nor the exponential rule (constant base) applies to xˣ, but this method does.
