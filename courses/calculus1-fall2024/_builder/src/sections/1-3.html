Calculus measures angles in radians. With radians, the six trigonometric functions become functions of a real number t, defined from the point where the angle t meets the unit circle. In this section we review radian measure, the six functions and where they are undefined, the special values, the basic identities, and the graphs with their periods.

Learning objectives Convert between degrees and radians; define sine and cosine from the unit circle; define tangent, cotangent, secant, and cosecant and find where they are undefined; recall the special values; use the Pythagorean identities; recognize the six graphs and their periods.

01 · Radian measure

Two units for one rotation

One degree (1°) is 1/360 of a full rotation, so a full circle is 360°. One radian is the central angle whose arc has the same length as the radius. A full circle is 2π radians, so 180° = π radians and 1 radian = 180°/π ≈ 57.3°.

  • Degrees → radians: multiply by π/180°.
  • Radians → degrees: multiply by 180°/π.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • Simplify the fraction: 45° = 45π/180 = π/4, not 45π/180.
  • No degree sign on radians: write 5π/6 or 5π/6 rad, never 5π/6°.
  • “2” means 2 radians (about 114.6°), not 2°.

02 · Sine and cosine on the unit circle

Definitions · Unit circle, sine, and cosine

The unit circle has center (0, 0) and radius 1; its equation is x² + y² = 1.

If t is a real number and the terminal side of the angle t meets the unit circle at the point (x, y), then cos t = x and sin t = y. So every point on the unit circle can be written as (cos t, sin t).

03 · The six trigonometric functions

Definitions · The other four functions

If (x, y) is the point where the terminal side of t meets the unit circle, then

tangenttan t = y/x, x ≠ 0
cotangentcot t = x/y, y ≠ 0
secantsec t = 1/x, x ≠ 0
cosecantcsc t = 1/y, y ≠ 0

When a denominator is 0 the function is undefined at that angle; for example, tan(π/2) and sec(π/2) are undefined because x = 0.

05 · Trigonometric identities

Basic identities
reciprocal and quotientsec t = 1/cos t, csc t = 1/sin t
tan t = sin t/cos t, cot t = cos t/sin t = 1/tan t
Pythagoreansin²t + cos²t = 1
1 + tan²t = sec²t
1 + cot²t = csc²t

Divide sin²t + cos²t = 1 by cos²t to get 1 + tan²t = sec²t, and by sin²t to get 1 + cot²t = csc²t. Sine and tangent are odd (sin(−t) = −sin t); cosine is even (cos(−t) = cos t).

INTERACTIVE · FIND THE MISSING VALUE

If you know one of sin t, cos t and the quadrant of t, the identity sin²t + cos²t = 1 gives the other. The first preset is cos t = 7/25 with t in Quadrant IV.

06 · Graphs and periods

Periodic functions

A function f is periodic with period P > 0 if f(x + P) = f(x) for all x, and P is the smallest such number. Sine, cosine, secant, and cosecant have period 2π; tangent and cotangent have period π. The graphs of tangent and secant have vertical asymptotes where cos x = 0, and those of cotangent and cosecant where sin x = 0.

INTERACTIVE · UNROLL THE UNIT CIRCLE
