The derivative y′ = f′(x) = limΔx→0 Δy/Δx measures how fast y changes compared with x. In this section we use that idea in three settings: estimating an amount of change, describing motion (position, velocity, speed, acceleration), and marginal rates in economics.

Learning objectives Estimate an amount of change with f(a + h) ≈ f(a) + f′(a)h; relate position, velocity, speed, and acceleration; decide when an object moves forward or backward; decide when its velocity increases or decreases and when it speeds up or slows down; interpret marginal cost and revenue.

01 · Amount of change and average rate

Estimating change with the derivative

The amount of change of f from a to a + h is f(a + h) − f(a), and the average rate of change is (f(a + h) − f(a))/h. For small h the average rate is close to f′(a), so

f(a + h) − f(a) ≈ f′(a) · h, that is, f(a + h) ≈ f(a) + f′(a)h.

02 · Position, velocity, speed, and acceleration

Motion on a line
positions(t), the location at time t
velocityv(t) = s′(t) (instantaneous, not average)
speed|v(t)|
accelerationa(t) = v′(t) = s″(t)
  • Moving forward (s increasing) ⟺ v(t) > 0; moving backward ⟺ v(t) < 0.
  • Velocity increasing ⟺ a(t) > 0; velocity decreasing ⟺ a(t) < 0.
  • Speeding up ⟺ v and a have the same sign; slowing down ⟺ v and a have opposite signs.
BE CAREFUL
  • Velocity increasing is not the same as speeding up. If v = −5 and then v = −2, the velocity increased but the speed dropped from 5 to 2: the object slowed down.
  • For s(t) = 4t/(t² + 4), t ≥ 0: v = −4(t² − 4)/(t² + 4)² and a = 8t(t² − 12)/(t² + 4)³. On (0, 2) v > 0 and a < 0: slowing down. On (2, 2√3) both are negative: speeding up. On (2√3, ∞) v < 0, a > 0: slowing down.

03 · Marginal cost and revenue

Marginal functions

If C(x) is the cost of producing x items, the marginal cost is C′(x). Since C(x + 1) − C(x) ≈ C′(x) · 1, the marginal cost approximates the cost of producing one more item. Marginal revenue R′(x) and marginal profit P′(x) are defined the same way.
