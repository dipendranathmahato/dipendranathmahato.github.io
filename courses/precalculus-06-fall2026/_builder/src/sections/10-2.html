An ellipse keeps the sum of the distances to its two foci constant. Keep the difference constant instead and the curve splits into two separate branches: a hyperbola. The same letters a, b, and c appear, but now c is the largest, and two new guide lines, the asymptotes, shape the graph.

Learning objectives Describe a hyperbola by its foci, vertices, co-vertices, and asymptotes; use c² = a² + b²; write equations of hyperbolas centered at the origin in standard form; find the features of a hyperbola from its equation.

01 · The difference of two distances

Hyperbola

A hyperbola is the set of all points P in a plane so that the difference of the distances from P to two fixed points, the foci, is a positive constant: |PF₁ − PF₂| = 2a.

  • The transverse axis joins the two vertices; the foci lie on its extension. The conjugate axis is perpendicular to it and its endpoints are the co-vertices.
  • a = distance from the center to each vertex, b = to each co-vertex, c = to each focus, with c > a and c > b.
  • The central rectangle has sides through the vertices and co-vertices. Its diagonals extend to the two asymptotes, lines through the center that the branches approach.
  • The transverse axis has length 2a and the conjugate axis has length 2b.

c² = a² + b²

02 · Writing the equation and the asymptotes

Standard equation of a hyperbola with center (0, 0)
transverse axis along the x-axisx²/a² − y²/b² = 1
vertices (±a, 0), co-vertices (0, ±b)
foci (±c, 0)
asymptotes y = ±(b/a)x
transverse axis along the y-axisy²/a² − x²/b² = 1
vertices (0, ±a), co-vertices (±b, 0)
foci (0, ±c)
asymptotes y = ±(a/b)x

a² is always under the positive term, whether or not it is the larger number.

03 · Reading the features from the equation

From the equation to the graph
  1. The variable in the positive term names the transverse axis; its denominator is a². The other denominator is b².
  2. Find c from c² = a² + b².
  3. List the vertices, co-vertices, and foci, and write the asymptotes y = ±(b/a)x or y = ±(a/b)x.
  4. To sketch: draw the central rectangle, extend its diagonals as the asymptotes, then draw each branch from a vertex toward the asymptotes.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • Ellipse habits do not carry over. For a hyperbola, c² = a² + b² (not a² − b²), and a² sits under the positive term even when it is the smaller denominator, as in x²/4 − y²/25 = 1.
  • Check which slope you need. Along x the asymptotes are y = ±(b/a)x; along y they are y = ±(a/b)x. Both are "rise over run" of the central rectangle's diagonal.
