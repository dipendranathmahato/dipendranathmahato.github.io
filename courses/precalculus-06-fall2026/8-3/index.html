Where we are coming from

In 8.8 · Vectors, a vector has a magnitude and a direction and can be added, scaled, and dotted.

A vector has a length and a direction. Using that same pair to name points gives polar coordinates: instead of walking x units across and y units up, turn by an angle θ and walk a distance r. Every point has infinitely many polar names, and the equations x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, and x² + y² = r² translate between the two systems.

Learning objectives Plot points using polar coordinates; convert from polar coordinates to rectangular coordinates; convert from rectangular coordinates to polar coordinates; transform equations between polar and rectangular forms.

01 · Plotting points on the polar grid

Polar coordinates (r, θ)
  • The pole is the origin and the polar axis is the positive x-axis. The grid has circles r = 1, 2, 3, … and rays at the special angles.
  • Measure θ first: counterclockwise from the polar axis if θ > 0, clockwise if θ < 0.
  • If r > 0, go r units along the terminal side. If r < 0, go |r| units along the ray opposite the terminal side.
  • (r, θ), (r, θ + 2π), and (−r, θ + π) all name the same point.

02 · Polar to rectangular

Conversion

x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ

INTERACTIVE · (r, θ) → (x, y)

Pick a preset or type your own r and θ. Type θ in radians, for example 3π/2 or 2.

03 · Rectangular to polar

Conversion

r² = x² + y², tan θ = y/x

The calculator's tan⁻¹(y/x) is always between −π/2 and π/2 (Quadrants I and IV). If the point is in Quadrant II or III, either add π to the angle (keeping r > 0) or keep the angle and use a negative r.

INTERACTIVE · DRAG THE POINT (x, y)

Pick a preset point or drag your own.

04 · Converting equations

Substitutions

Use x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, and x² + y² = r². To go from polar to rectangular it often helps to multiply both sides by r first (to create r cos θ, r sin θ, or r²), or to rewrite sec θ and csc θ as 1/cos θ and 1/sin θ.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · Polar Coordinates

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Convert (7, 5), (−3, 1), (−2, −6), and (9, −4) to polar coordinates with r > 0.

    Show answer

    (√74, ≈ 0.62); (√10, ≈ 2.82); (2√10, ≈ 4.39); (√97, ≈ −0.42), which can also be written (√97, ≈ 5.86).

  2. Write 2x − y = 6, 3x² + 5y = 1, and x² + y² = 8y in polar form.

    Show answer

    r(2 cos θ − sin θ) = 6, so r = 6/(2 cos θ − sin θ); 3r²cos²θ + 5r sin θ = 1; r² = 8r sin θ, so r = 8 sin θ.

  3. Write r = 7 sin θ and r = 2 csc θ in rectangular form.

    Show answer

    r² = 7r sin θ gives x² + y² = 7y, that is x² + (y − 7/2)² = 49/4. r = 2/sin θ gives r sin θ = 2, that is y = 2.
