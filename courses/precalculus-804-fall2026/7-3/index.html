Where we are coming from

In 7.2 · Sum and Difference Identities, the sum and difference formulas give exact values from two special angles.

Put β = α in the sum formulas and you get the double-angle formulas: sin(α + α), cos(α + α), and tan(α + α). Solve one of the cosine forms for sin²α or cos²α and you get the half-angle formulas. With these we can find exact values for angles like 600°, 15°, 22.5°, and 7π/8, and for 2θ or θ/2 when θ is given by a ratio and a quadrant.

Learning objectives Use double-angle formulas to find exact values; use half-angle formulas to find exact values.

01 · Double-angle formulas

Double-angle formulas
sinesin(2α) = 2 sin α cos α
cosine (three forms)cos(2α) = cos²α − sin²α
= 1 − 2 sin²α
= 2 cos²α − 1
tangenttan(2α) = 2 tan α / (1 − tan²α)

The second and third forms of cos(2α) come from replacing cos²α by 1 − sin²α, or sin²α by 1 − cos²α. Pick the form that uses the value you are given.

02 · Half-angle formulas

Half-angle formulas
sinesin(α/2) = ±√((1 − cos α)/2)
cosinecos(α/2) = ±√((1 + cos α)/2)
tangenttan(α/2) = ±√((1 − cos α)/(1 + cos α))
= sin α/(1 + cos α)
= (1 − cos α)/sin α

The ± sign is not a choice of two answers: pick + or − from the quadrant where α/2 terminates. The last two tangent forms need no sign decision.

INTERACTIVE · HALF OF A SPECIAL ANGLE
COMMON MISTAKES
  • sin(α/2) ≠ ½ sin α. For α = 60°: sin 30° = 1/2, but ½ sin 60° = √3/4.
  • Match the sign in the formula: cos(α/2) uses 1 + cos α; sin(α/2) uses 1 − cos α. Mixing them up is the most common error.

03 · Half of an angle given by a ratio

If θ is given by a ratio and an interval, first find cos θ, then decide where θ/2 lies by halving the interval. For example, cos θ = 1/5 with 3π/2 < θ < 2π gives 3π/4 < θ/2 < π, in Quadrant II.

INTERACTIVE · HALVE THE INTERVAL, THEN THE ANGLE
POINT TO REMEMBER

For cos θ = 1/5 with 3π/2 < θ < 2π, cos(θ/2) uses 1 + cos θ: cos(θ/2) = −√((1 + 1/5)/2) = −√(3/5) = −√15/5 ≈ −0.775. A calculator check agrees: θ = 2π − cos⁻¹(1/5) ≈ 4.914, and cos(θ/2) ≈ −0.775. (The value −√10/5 comes from 1 − cos θ, which gives sin(θ/2) up to sign.)

Check your understanding · Double-Angle and Half-Angle Formulas

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. If tan θ = 4/3 and π < θ < 3π/2, find sin(2θ), cos(2θ), and tan(2θ).

    Show answer

    sin θ = −4/5 and cos θ = −3/5. sin 2θ = 2(−4/5)(−3/5) = 24/25; cos 2θ = 9/25 − 16/25 = −7/25; tan 2θ = 2(4/3)/(1 − 16/9) = −24/7.

  2. Use half-angle formulas to find sin(7π/8), cos 75°, and tan 15° exactly.

    Show answer

    sin(7π/8) = +√((1 − cos(7π/4))/2) = √(2 − √2)/2. cos 75° = +√((1 + cos 150°)/2) = √(2 − √3)/2. tan 15° = (1 − cos 30°)/sin 30° = 2 − √3.

  3. If sin θ = −5/13 and π < θ < 3π/2, find sin(θ/2) and cos(θ/2).

    Show answer

    cos θ = −12/13; π/2 < θ/2 < 3π/4 (Quadrant II). sin(θ/2) = +√((1 + 12/13)/2) = 5√26/26; cos(θ/2) = −√((1 − 12/13)/2) = −√26/26.
