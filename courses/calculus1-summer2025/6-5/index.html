Where we are coming from

In 6.1 · Areas between Curves, we computed areas between curves.

Integrals add up quantities that change from point to point. A rod whose density varies, a disk that is denser near the center, a spring that pushes back harder the more it is stretched, and water that must be lifted different distances from different depths: in each case we cut the object or the motion into small pieces, approximate each piece by a constant, add, and take the limit. The result is a definite integral.

Learning objectives Find the mass of a thin rod from a linear density; find the mass of a disk from a radial density; compute the work done by a variable force, including springs (Hooke's law); set up and compute the work needed to pump liquid out of a tank.

01 · Mass of a thin rod

Linear density

A thin rod lies along the x-axis from x = a to x = b. If its density (mass per unit length) is a constant ρ, its mass is ρ(b − a). If the density ρ(x) changes from point to point, cut [a, b] into n pieces; the piece [xᵢ₋₁, xᵢ] has mass mᵢ ≈ ρ(xᵢ*)Δx. Adding gives a Riemann sum, and in the limit

m = limn→∞ Σ ρ(xᵢ*)Δx = ∫ab ρ(x) dx.

Example: a rod on [π/2, π] with density ρ(x) = sin x has mass ∫π/2π sin x dx = [−cos x]π/2π = 1 − 0 = 1.

02 · Mass of a disk with radial density

Thin rings

A thin disk of radius r is centered at the origin, and its density depends only on the distance x from the center: ρ(x) (mass per unit area). Cut [0, r] into pieces; each piece sweeps out a thin ring with inner radius xᵢ₋₁ and outer radius xᵢ, of area π(xᵢ² − xᵢ₋₁²) = π(xᵢ + xᵢ₋₁)Δx ≈ 2πxᵢ*Δx. Its mass is about 2πxᵢ*ρ(xᵢ*)Δx, so

m = ∫0r 2πx ρ(x) dx.

Example: ρ(x) = 3x + 2 and r = 2: m = 2π∫02 (3x² + 2x) dx = 2π[x³ + x²]02 = 24π.

03 · Work done by a variable force

Work

A constant force F moving an object a distance d does work W = Fd. If the force F(x) varies as the object moves along the x-axis from a to b, then

W = ∫ab F(x) dx.

Hooke's law: the force needed to stretch or compress a spring x units from its natural (equilibrium) length is F(x) = kx, where k > 0 is the spring constant.

Example: a force of 30 N stretches a spring 5 cm = 0.05 m, so 30 = k(0.05) and k = 600 N/m. To stretch it an additional 20 cm (from 0.05 m to 0.25 m): W = ∫0.050.25 600x dx = 300[x²]0.050.25 = 300(0.0625 − 0.0025) = 18 J.

04 · Work done in pumping

A working rule
  1. Sketch the tank and choose a coordinate: here x is the depth below the top.
  2. Find the volume of a representative thin layer of liquid at depth xᵢ*.
  3. Multiply by the weight density of the liquid to get the force needed to lift it (water: 62.4 lb/ft³).
  4. Find the distance the layer must be lifted.
  5. Multiply force by distance to get the work for that layer.
  6. Add the work for all layers: a Riemann sum.
  7. Take the limit to get an integral, and evaluate it.

Example: a tank is an inverted cone of height 12 ft and top radius 4 ft, full of water. Water is pumped over the top edge until 4 ft of water remain. A layer at depth x has radius r with r/(12 − x) = 4/12 (similar triangles), so r = 4 − x/3; its volume is about π(4 − x/3)²Δx and its weight 62.4π(4 − x/3)²Δx, and it is lifted x ft. The layers to remove run from x = 0 to x = 8, so

W = ∫08 62.4π x(4 − x/3)² dx = 62.4π · 512/3 = 10,649.6π ≈ 33,457 ft-lb.
