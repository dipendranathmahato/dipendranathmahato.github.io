Where we are coming from

In 4.6 · Limits at Infinity and Asymptotes, we computed limits at infinity and found horizontal asymptotes.

Limits such as limx→1 (ln x)/(2x − 2) and limx→∞ eˣ/x lead to the forms 0/0 and ∞/∞, which say nothing about the answer by themselves. L’Hôpital’s rule replaces the quotient by the quotient of the derivatives, which is often easy to evaluate. Other indeterminate forms, 0 · ∞, ∞ − ∞, 1∞, 0⁰, and ∞⁰, are first rewritten as one of these two.

Learning objectives Recognize the indeterminate forms; apply L’Hôpital’s rule to 0/0 and ∞/∞, repeatedly if needed; rewrite 0 · ∞ and ∞ − ∞ as quotients; use logarithms for 1∞, 0⁰, and ∞⁰; compare the growth rates of logarithms, powers, and exponentials.

01 · L’Hôpital’s rule

L’Hôpital’s rule

Suppose f and g are differentiable near c (except possibly at c), g′(x) ≠ 0 near c, and limx→c f(x) = limx→c g(x) = 0 or both limits are ±∞. Then

limx→c f(x)/g(x) = limx→c f′(x)/g′(x)

if the right side exists (or is ±∞). The same holds for one-sided limits and for c = ±∞. Basic indeterminate forms: 0/0 and ∞/∞. Other forms: 0 · ∞, 1∞, 0⁰, ∞ − ∞, ∞⁰.

BE CAREFUL
  • It looks like a fraction, but it is not the quotient rule: differentiate the numerator and the denominator separately.
  • Check the form first. limx→0 (x + 1)/(x + 2) = 1/2 by substitution; using the rule there would give the wrong answer 1.
  • Do not apply the rule directly to the other indeterminate forms: rewrite them first.
INTERACTIVE · WHY THE RULE WORKS

Near c, both f and g look like their tangent lines through (c, 0). The ratio of two lines through the same point is the ratio of their slopes.

05 · Growth rates

Which grows faster?

f grows more rapidly than g as x → ∞ if lim f(x)/g(x) = ∞ (equivalently g/f → 0). L’Hôpital’s rule shows that for every p > 0 and every n, ln x ≪ xᵖ ≪ eˣ: lim (ln x)/xᵖ = 0 and lim xⁿ/eˣ = 0.

Check your understanding · L’Hôpital’s Rule

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Find limx→0 (sin 3x)/(tan 4x).

    Show answer

    0/0, so by L’Hôpital: lim 3 cos 3x/(4 sec²4x) = 3/4.

  2. Find limx→∞ x e−x².

    Show answer

    0 · ∞. Rewrite as x/ex² (∞/∞) and apply the rule: 1/(2x ex²) → 0.

  3. Find limx→∞ x1/x.

    Show answer

    ∞⁰. Let y = x1/x, so ln y = (ln x)/x → (1/x)/1 = 0 by L’Hôpital. Hence y → e⁰ = 1.
