Where we are coming from

In 5.4 · Integration Formulas and the Net Change Theorem, we used the net change theorem to integrate rates.

The chain rule says d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))·g′(x). Read backwards, it says that an integrand of the form f′(g(x))·g′(x) has antiderivative f(g(x)). Substitution organizes this: name the inside function u = g(x), replace g′(x) dx by du, and the integral becomes a simpler one in u.

Learning objectives Recognize an integrand of the form f′(g(x))g′(x); choose a substitution u = g(x) and rewrite the integral in u, adjusting constants; return to the original variable; evaluate definite integrals by substitution, either back-substituting or changing the limits.

01 · Reversing the chain rule

Substitution

From the chain rule, ∫ f′(g(x)) g′(x) dx = f(g(x)) + C. Put u = g(x); then du/dx = g′(x), that is, du = g′(x) dx (the differential of u), and

∫ f′(g(x)) g′(x) dx = ∫ f′(u) du = f(u) + C = f(g(x)) + C.

  1. Choose u, usually the "inside" function whose derivative also appears (up to a constant factor).
  2. Rewrite the whole integral in terms of u and du; no x may remain.
  3. Integrate with respect to u.
  4. Substitute back u = g(x).

03 · Substitution in definite integrals

Two methods

Method 1: find an antiderivative in x first (substitute back), then use the original limits. Method 2: change the limits along with the variable: if u = g(x), then

∫ab f(g(x)) g′(x) dx = ∫g(a)g(b) f(u) du,

and never return to x. Example: ∫01 3/√(6x + 5) dx with u = 6x + 5, du = 6 dx: x = 0 gives u = 5 and x = 1 gives u = 11, so the integral is ∫511 ½u−1/2 du = u1/2|511 = √11 − √5.

INTERACTIVE · TWO REGIONS, ONE AREA

The region under the original integrand on [a, b] and the region under the new integrand on [g(a), g(b)] look different but have the same area.

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Compute ∫ (7x + 9)⁸ dx.

    Show answer

    u = 7x + 9, du = 7 dx, so dx = du/7: ∫ u⁸ du/7 = u⁹/63 + C = (7x + 9)⁹/63 + C.

  2. Compute ∫ cos²θ sin θ dθ.

    Show answer

    u = cos θ, du = −sin θ dθ: ∫ u²(−du) = −u³/3 + C = −cos³θ/3 + C.

  3. Evaluate ∫01 3/√(6x + 5) dx by changing the limits.

    Show answer

    u = 6x + 5, du = 6 dx; u runs from 5 to 11. ∫511 (3/√u)(du/6) = ∫511 ½u−1/2 du = √u |511 = √11 − √5.
