The derivatives of sin⁻¹x, tan⁻¹x, and sec⁻¹x are algebraic functions: 1/√(1 − x²), 1/(1 + x²), and 1/(x√(x² − 1)). Read backwards, they let us integrate expressions with a² − u², a² + u², and u² − a², which appear often and have no simpler antiderivatives.

Learning objectives Use the three integration formulas that give inverse trigonometric functions, with a constant a; match an integrand to the right form, factoring out constants or substituting when needed; evaluate definite integrals with exact values such as π/6 and 5π/12.

01 · The three formulas

Integrals that give inverse trigonometric functions (a > 0)
1 · sine∫ du/√(a² − u²) = sin⁻¹(u/a) + C
2 · tangent∫ du/(a² + u²) = (1/a) tan⁻¹(u/a) + C
3 · secant∫ du/(u√(u² − a²)) = (1/a) sec⁻¹(|u|/a) + C
remember the rangessin⁻¹: [−π/2, π/2]; tan⁻¹: (−π/2, π/2): tan⁻¹x is the unique θ in (−π/2, π/2) with tan θ = x

Each formula can be checked by differentiating; for example d/du [(1/a) tan⁻¹(u/a)] = (1/a) · (1/a)/(1 + u²/a²) = 1/(a² + u²).
