Where we are coming from

In 2.4 · Continuity, we defined continuity and used the Intermediate Value Theorem.

The average rate of change of f between two points is the slope of a secant line. To get the rate of change at a single point, move the second point along the curve toward the first. The limit of the secant slopes is the slope of the tangent line: the derivative.

Learning objectives Write the difference quotient for the slope of a secant line; define the derivative f′(a) as a limit in two equivalent forms; compute f′(a) from the definition; find the equation of the tangent line at a point; interpret f′(a) as an instantaneous rate of change.

01 · From secant slope to derivative

Definition · The derivative at a point

The slope of the secant line through (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)) is the average rate of change Δy/Δx = (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a). Let f be defined on an open interval containing a. The derivative of f at a (the slope of the tangent line) is

f′(a) = limx→a (f(x) − f(a))/(x − a) = limh→0 (f(a + h) − f(a))/h,

provided the limit exists. It is the instantaneous rate of change of f with respect to x at x = a. (Put x = a + h to pass from one form to the other.)

02 · Computing f′(a) from the definition

Strategy

Write the difference quotient, simplify until the factor (x − a) or h cancels, then take the limit by substitution. For f(x) = x² at a = 3: (x² − 9)/(x − 3) = (x + 3)(x − 3)/(x − 3) = x + 3 → 6, so f′(3) = 6.

INTERACTIVE · WORKED EXAMPLES, ONE STEP AT A TIME

03 · The equation of the tangent line

Tangent line at (a, f(a))

The tangent line passes through (a, f(a)) with slope f′(a). In point-slope form: y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a). For f(x) = x² at x = 3 the point is (3, 9) and the slope is 6, so y − 9 = 6(x − 3), that is, y = 6x − 9.

INTERACTIVE · BUILD THE TANGENT LINE

04 · The derivative as a rate of change

Units of f′(a)

If y = f(x), then f′(a) is measured in units of y per unit of x. If s(t) is a position in meters at time t seconds, s′(a) is the velocity in meters per second. If C(x) is the cost in dollars of making x items, C′(a) is the rate at which cost grows, in dollars per item.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · Defining the Derivative

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Use the definition to find f′(3) for f(x) = x², and the tangent line at x = 3.

    Show answer

    f′(3) = limx→3 (x² − 9)/(x − 3) = limx→3 (x + 3) = 6. The point is (3, 9), so y − 9 = 6(x − 3), or y = 6x − 9.

  2. Using limh→0 (eʰ − 1)/h = 1, show that the derivative of f(x) = eˣ is eˣ.

    Show answer

    f′(x) = limh→0 (ex + h − eˣ)/h = limh→0 eˣ(eʰ − 1)/h = eˣ · limh→0 (eʰ − 1)/h = eˣ · 1 = eˣ.

  3. For f(x) = 1/x, find f′(2) from the definition.

    Show answer

    (f(2 + h) − f(2))/h = (1/(2 + h) − 1/2)/h = (2 − (2 + h))/(2h(2 + h)) = −1/(2(2 + h)) → −1/4. So f′(2) = −1/4.
