Chapter 1

Functions and Graphs

5 sections · Week 1

SECTION 1.1Week 1Review of FunctionsFunction notation, domain and range, interval notation, graphs, the vertical line test, piecewise functions, and composition. What makes a rule a function, and how do we read one from a formula, a table, or a graph?SECTION 1.2Week 1Basic Classes of FunctionsLinear functions and slope, polynomials, power and rational functions, algebraic and transcendental functions, and shifts, stretches, and reflections. Which families of functions will we meet again and again, and how do transformations move them?SECTION 1.3Week 1Trigonometric FunctionsRadians, the unit circle, the six trigonometric functions, identities, and the graphs of sine, cosine, and tangent. How do angles on the unit circle turn into the six trigonometric functions?SECTION 1.4Week 1Inverse FunctionsOne-to-one functions, the horizontal line test, finding inverses, reflection across y = x, restricted domains, and inverse trigonometric functions. When can we undo a function, and what does the graph of the inverse look like?SECTION 1.5Week 1Exponential and Logarithmic FunctionsExponential functions and their laws, the number e, logarithms as inverses, properties of logarithms, and solving exponential equations. What kind of growth multiplies instead of adds, and how do logarithms undo it?
Chapter 2

Limits

4 sections · Weeks 1–2

SECTION 2.1Week 1A Preview of CalculusThe tangent problem (secant slopes approaching a tangent), instantaneous velocity, and the area problem (rectangles under a curve). How can a sequence of approximations lead to an exact slope or an exact area?SECTION 2.2Week 1The Limit of a FunctionLimits from tables and graphs, one-sided limits, when a limit does not exist, infinite limits, and vertical asymptotes. What value does f(x) approach as x gets close to a, even if f(a) is undefined?SECTION 2.3Week 2The Limit LawsLimit laws, direct substitution, 0/0 forms handled by factoring, rationalizing, and simplifying, and the squeeze theorem. How can we compute limits exactly instead of guessing from tables?SECTION 2.4Week 2ContinuityContinuity at a point, removable, jump, and infinite discontinuities, continuity on intervals, and the Intermediate Value Theorem. What does it mean to draw a graph without lifting the pencil?
Chapter 3

Derivatives

7 sections · Weeks 2–3

SECTION 3.1Week 2Defining the DerivativeThe difference quotient, the derivative at a point as a limit, tangent lines, and the derivative as an instantaneous rate of change. How do secant slopes become the slope of the tangent line?SECTION 3.2Week 2The Derivative as a Functionf′(x) from the limit definition, graphing f′ from f, where a function fails to be differentiable, and higher derivatives. If every point has a slope, what does the slope function look like?SECTION 3.3Week 2Differentiation RulesThe constant, power, sum, and constant-multiple rules, the product and quotient rules, and combining the rules. How can we find derivatives without computing a limit every time?SECTION 3.5Week 2Derivatives of Trigonometric FunctionsThe derivatives of sin x and cos x, the other four trigonometric functions, and higher derivatives of sine and cosine. Why is the slope of sin x given by cos x?SECTION 3.6Week 2The Chain RuleThe chain rule for compositions, inside and outside functions, powers and trigonometric functions, chain rule with tables of values, and the derivatives of the inverse trigonometric functions. How do we differentiate a function inside a function?SECTION 3.9Week 3Derivatives of Exponential and Logarithmic FunctionsDerivatives of eˣ and bˣ, of ln x and logb x, the chain rule with exponentials and logarithms, and logarithmic differentiation. Which function is its own derivative, and what is the derivative of ln x?SECTION 3.4Week 3Derivatives as Rates of ChangeAmount of change and average rate, position, velocity, speed, and acceleration, speeding up and slowing down, and marginal cost. What does the derivative tell us about motion and other changing quantities?

Applications of Derivatives

Chapter 5

Integration

7 sections · Weeks 4–5

SECTION 5.1Week 4Approximating AreasSigma notation and sum formulas, left, right, and midpoint approximations, and the exact area as a limit of sums. Which integrals give inverse trigonometric functions?

CHAPTER 0 COURSE FOUNDATIONS

Introduction to Calculus I

Calculus studies two key problems: how to find an instantaneous rate of change, and how to find a total accumulation (the area under a curve). Both are solved with what you know from high-school mathematics plus one new idea, the limit. This is a guide to the expectations, grading, and exams for MATH 1210-01 in Summer 2025.

Learning outcomes

Analyze functions given graphically, numerically, analytically, or verbally; compute limits; interpret derivatives and integrals as limiting processes; use the derivative as a rate of change and a local linear approximation; interpret the definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums and as the net accumulation of a rate; relate derivatives and integrals through the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus; model physical situations with functions, derivatives, and integrals; and communicate solutions in clear, well-organized sentences.
