Where we are coming from

In 1.5 · Exponential and Logarithmic Functions, we studied exponential growth and the logarithms that undo it.

Calculus grew out of two problems. The tangent problem asks for the slope of the line that just touches a curve at a point. The area problem asks for the area of a region with a curved boundary. Neither can be solved with one computation: we approximate, improve the approximation, and ask what number the approximations approach. That number is a limit.

Learning objectives Describe the tangent problem and approximate a tangent slope with secant slopes; describe the area problem and approximate an area with rectangles; relate average and instantaneous velocity; explain why both problems lead to limits.

01 · The tangent problem

From secant lines to the tangent line

For a straight line y = mx + c through (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), the slope is m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). For a curve y = f(x) and a point P = (a, f(a)) on it, we want the slope of the tangent line at P.

  1. Choose another point Q = (a + h, f(a + h)) on the curve.
  2. Draw the secant line through P and Q. Its slope is msec = (f(a + h) − f(a))/h.
  3. Move Q toward P along the curve (let h → 0).
  4. The secant slopes approach the slope of the tangent line. This is the idea of a limit.

02 · Average and instantaneous velocity

Velocity is a slope

If s(t) is the position of an object at time t, its average velocity on [a, a + h] is (s(a + h) − s(a))/h: the slope of a secant line of the position graph. The instantaneous velocity at t = a is the number these averages approach as h → 0: the slope of the tangent line.

INTERACTIVE · A BALL THROWN UPWARD

A ball is thrown straight up; its height after t seconds is s(t) = −16t² + 64t feet. Shrink the time interval to find the velocity at one instant.

03 · The area problem

From rectangles to area

We know the areas of rectangles (ab), triangles (½bh), trapezoids (½(a + b)h), and circles (πr²). To find the area under a curve y = f(x) ≥ 0 from x = a to x = b, divide [a, b] into n strips of width Δx = (b − a)/n, approximate each strip by a rectangle, and add. The narrower the rectangles, the better the approximation; the area is the limit of these sums as n → ∞.

04 · Where the two problems lead

the tangent problem → derivativesslopes and rates of change: velocity and acceleration of a moving particle; optimization (finding maxima and minima)
the area problem → integralsareas and volumes; total accumulation, such as distance traveled from a velocity, or total change from a rate

Both problems are answered by limits, which we define in Section 2.2. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Chapter 5) shows that the two problems are inverse to each other.

Check your understanding · A Preview of Calculus

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. For f(x) = x², find the slope of the secant line through P = (1, 1) and Q = (1 + h, (1 + h)²). What happens as h → 0?

    Show answer

    msec = ((1 + h)² − 1)/h = (2h + h²)/h = 2 + h for h ≠ 0. As h → 0 the slopes approach 2, the slope of the tangent line at (1, 1).

  2. For s(t) = −16t² + 64t, find the average velocity on [1, 1 + h] and the instantaneous velocity at t = 1.

    Show answer

    s(1 + h) − s(1) = −16(1 + 2h + h²) + 64 + 64h − 48 = 32h − 16h², so the average velocity is 32 − 16h ft/s. As h → 0 it approaches 32 ft/s.

  3. Use 4 right-endpoint rectangles to estimate the area under y = x² on [0, 2].

    Show answer

    Δx = 0.5 and the right endpoints are 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2: R₄ = 0.5(0.25 + 1 + 2.25 + 4) = 3.75. The exact area is 8/3 ≈ 2.667; more rectangles give better estimates.
