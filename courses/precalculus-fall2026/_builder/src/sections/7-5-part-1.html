Solving a trigonometric equation has two stages. First, solve it like an algebraic equation for the trigonometric function: isolate sin θ, cos θ, or tan θ. Second, find every angle with that value, using a reference angle and the signs in each quadrant. Because the functions repeat, an equation usually has two solutions in [0, 2π) and infinitely many solutions overall.

Learning objectives Solve linear trigonometric equations in sine and cosine; solve equations involving a single trigonometric function; solve trigonometric equations using a calculator.

01 · Where are the solutions?

Solutions of f(θ) = k
  • sin θ = k: the points of the unit circle at height y = k. For −1 < k < 1 there are two in [0, 2π).
  • cos θ = k: the points with x-coordinate k.
  • tan θ = k: the two ends of the line through the origin with slope k.
  • Sine and cosine repeat every 2π and tangent every π, so all solutions are found by adding 2kπ (or kπ for tangent), k an integer.
  • On a graph, the solutions are the x-intercepts of y = f(θ) − k.

02 · Solving a linear trigonometric equation

Procedure
  1. Isolate the trigonometric function: a·f(θ) + b = c ⟹ f(θ) = (c − b)/a.
  2. Find the reference angle from the absolute value (special angle or calculator: f⁻¹ of the positive value).
  3. Use the sign to pick the quadrants and write the angles there.
  4. Keep the solutions in the given interval, or add 2kπ (kπ for tangent) for the general solution.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • sin⁻¹ gives only one angle. sin⁻¹(−3/5) ≈ −0.64 is not even in [0, 2π). Use the reference angle sin⁻¹(3/5) ≈ 0.64 and place it in Quadrants III and IV: π + 0.64 ≈ 3.79 and 2π − 0.64 ≈ 5.64.
  • Respect the interval. In [−π, π) the solutions of tan x = −1 are −π/4 and 3π/4, not 3π/4 and 7π/4.
