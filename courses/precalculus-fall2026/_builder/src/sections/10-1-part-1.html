Chapter 10 studies the conic sections: curves made by slicing a double cone with a plane. The first is the ellipse, an oval defined by two special points called foci. In this part we learn what a, b, and c measure, how they are related, and how to write the standard equation of an ellipse centered at the origin from a description of its features.

Learning objectives Describe an ellipse by its foci, vertices, and co-vertices; use a² = b² + c²; write equations of ellipses centered at the origin in standard form.

01 · The sum of two distances

Ellipse

An ellipse is the set of all points P in a plane so that the sum of the distances from P to two fixed points, the foci F₁ and F₂, is a constant. That constant is 2a.

  • The major axis is the longer axis of symmetry; its endpoints are the vertices. The minor axis is the shorter one; its endpoints are the co-vertices.
  • The center is the midpoint of both axes.
  • a = distance from the center to each vertex, b = distance to each co-vertex, c = distance to each focus, with a > b and a > c.
  • The major axis has length 2a and the minor axis has length 2b.

a² = b² + c²

02 · Two standard forms

Standard equation of an ellipse with center (0, 0)
major axis along the x-axisx²/a² + y²/b² = 1
vertices (±a, 0)
co-vertices (0, ±b)
foci (±c, 0)
major axis along the y-axisx²/b² + y²/a² = 1
vertices (0, ±a)
co-vertices (±b, 0)
foci (0, ±c)

a² is always the larger denominator, and it sits under the variable of the major axis.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • A co-vertex is on the minor axis. A co-vertex at (0, 2) means the minor axis is vertical, so the major axis is along the x-axis.
  • a² is the larger denominator, not necessarily the one under x². In x²/9 + y²/16 = 1, a² = 16 and the major axis is vertical.

03 · Writing the equation from its features

Strategy
  1. Decide the direction of the major axis from the given points.
  2. Read off what you know: a from a vertex or from the length 2a, b from a co-vertex, c from a focus.
  3. Find the missing square with a² = b² + c², or substitute a point on the graph into the equation.
  4. Put a² under the major-axis variable and b² under the other.
