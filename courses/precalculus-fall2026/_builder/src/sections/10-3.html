The ellipse and the hyperbola each have two foci. The last conic, the parabola, has one focus and one line, the directrix: every point on it is the same distance from both. One number, p, controls everything: where the focus is, where the directrix is, how wide the curve opens, and which way it faces.

Learning objectives Describe a parabola by its focus, directrix, vertex, and latus rectum; find these features from an equation with vertex at the origin; write equations of parabolas with vertex at the origin in standard form.

01 · Equally far from a point and a line

Parabola

A parabola is the set of all points in a plane that are the same distance from a fixed line, the directrix, and a fixed point not on it, the focus.

  • The vertex is the midpoint between the focus and the directrix. The axis of symmetry passes through the vertex and the focus, perpendicular to the directrix.
  • p is the directed distance from the vertex to the focus; the directrix is the same distance on the other side.
  • The latus rectum is the segment through the focus, parallel to the directrix, with endpoints on the parabola. Its length is |4p|.

02 · Reading the features from the equation

Standard equation of a parabola with vertex (0, 0)
axis of symmetry: the y-axisx² = 4py
focus (0, p), directrix y = −p
latus rectum endpoints (±2p, p)
opens up if p > 0, down if p < 0
axis of symmetry: the x-axisy² = 4px
focus (p, 0), directrix x = −p
latus rectum endpoints (p, ±2p)
opens right if p > 0, left if p < 0

If the squared variable has a coefficient, as in 2x² = −y, divide by it first to reach x² = 4py or y² = 4px. Then set the coefficient equal to 4p.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • The coefficient is 4p, not p. In x² = 7y, 4p = 7, so p = 7/4 and the focus is (0, 7/4), not (0, 7).
  • The directrix has the opposite sign. If the focus is (0, −1/8), the directrix is y = +1/8.

03 · Writing the equation from its features

Strategy (vertex at the origin)
  1. Decide the axis of symmetry: the focus is on it, and the directrix is perpendicular to it.
  2. Choose the form: x² = 4py for the y-axis, y² = 4px for the x-axis.
  3. Find p with its sign: from the focus (0, p) or (p, 0), from the directrix y = −p or x = −p, or from a latus rectum endpoint (±2p, p) or (p, ±2p).
  4. Substitute p and simplify 4p.
