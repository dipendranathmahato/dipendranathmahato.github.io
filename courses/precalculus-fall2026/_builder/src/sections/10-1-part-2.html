In Part I we built the equation of an ellipse from its features. Now we go the other way: start from an equation such as x²/9 + y²/16 = 1, read off a, b, and c, list the vertices, co-vertices, and foci, and draw the graph.

Learning objectives Identify the major axis from an equation in standard form; find the vertices, co-vertices, foci, and axis lengths; graph ellipses centered at the origin.

01 · Reading the features from the equation

From x²/(denominator) + y²/(denominator) = 1
  1. The larger denominator is a²; the smaller is b². The variable over a² names the major axis.
  2. Find c from c² = a² − b².
  3. Major axis along x: vertices (±a, 0), co-vertices (0, ±b), foci (±c, 0). Along y: vertices (0, ±a), co-vertices (±b, 0), foci (0, ±c).
  4. The major axis has length 2a and the minor axis has length 2b.

POINT TO REMEMBER

For x²/18 + y²/15 = 1, c² = 18 − 15 = 3, so c = √3 (not 3) and the foci are (±√3, 0). Check: c must be smaller than a = 3√2 ≈ 4.24, and √3 ≈ 1.73 is.

02 · Graphing in four steps

To graph an ellipse centered at the origin
  1. Plot the center (0, 0).
  2. Plot the two vertices, a units from the center along the major axis.
  3. Plot the two co-vertices, b units from the center along the minor axis.
  4. Draw a smooth oval through the four points. Then mark the foci, c units from the center on the major axis.

03 · Equations not yet in standard form

Divide to get 1 on the right

An equation like 4x² + 25y² = 100 is an ellipse, but it is not in standard form. Divide every term by the constant on the right: 4x²/100 + 25y²/100 = 1, which simplifies to x²/25 + y²/4 = 1. Then read the features as before.

INTERACTIVE · Ax² + By² = C

Use positive whole numbers A, B, and C.
