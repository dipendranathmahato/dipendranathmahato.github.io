Put an angle t in standard position on a circle of radius 1. Its terminal side crosses the circle at exactly one point (x, y). The two coordinates of that point are the two most important functions in trigonometry: cos t = x and sin t = y. Every fact in this section, from the Pythagorean identity to the signs in each quadrant, comes from that one picture.

Learning objectives Find the sine and cosine of 30° (π/6), 45° (π/4), and 60° (π/3); identify the domain and range of the sine and cosine functions; use reference angles to evaluate trigonometric functions.

01 · Sine and cosine on the unit circle

Definitions · Unit circle, sine, and cosine

The unit circle has center (0, 0) and radius 1; its equation is x² + y² = 1.

If t is a real number and the terminal side of the angle t meets the unit circle at the point (x, y), then cos t = x and sin t = y. So every point on the unit circle can be written as (cos t, sin t).

02 · The Pythagorean identity

Identity · cos²t + sin²t = 1

The point (cos t, sin t) lies on x² + y² = 1, so for every angle t, cos²t + sin²t = 1. If you know one of the two values and the quadrant of t, you can find the other: solve for the square, take the square root, and choose the sign that matches the quadrant.

Domain and range: y = cos t and y = sin t are defined for all real numbers, and their outputs always lie in [−1, 1], because the coordinates of a point on the unit circle are between −1 and 1.

04 · Using reference angles to find sine and cosine

Procedure · Values from the reference angle
  1. If t is not between 0 and 2π (0° and 360°), find its coterminal angle first.
  2. Find the reference angle t′ and the values cos t′ and sin t′.
  3. Find the quadrant of t and attach the signs: cos t is the x-coordinate and sin t is the y-coordinate.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • The reference angle only gives the size. cos 150° is not cos 30°: the x-coordinate in Quadrant II is negative, so cos 150° = −√3/2.
  • Measure to the x-axis, never to the y-axis. The reference angle of 120° is 60°, not 30°.
