The Law of Sines needs an angle together with its opposite side. When we know two sides and the angle between them (SAS), or all three sides (SSS), no such pair is available. The Law of Cosines handles these cases. It is the Pythagorean Theorem with a correction term that depends on the cosine of the angle.

Learning objectives Use the Law of Cosines to solve oblique triangles; solve applied problems using the Law of Cosines; use Heron's formula to find the area of a triangle.

01 · The Law of Cosines

Law of Cosines
to find a sidea² = b² + c² − 2bc cos α
b² = a² + c² − 2ac cos β
c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos γ
to find an angleα = cos⁻¹((b² + c² − a²)/(2bc))
β = cos⁻¹((a² + c² − b²)/(2ac))
γ = cos⁻¹((a² + b² − c²)/(2ab))

The square of a side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, minus twice their product times the cosine of the included angle.

INTERACTIVE · PYTHAGORAS PLUS A CORRECTION

Change the angle γ between sides a and b. At γ = 90° the correction is 0 and you get the Pythagorean Theorem; an acute γ makes c shorter, an obtuse γ makes it longer.

02 · SAS: two sides and the included angle

Procedure
  1. Use the Law of Cosines to find the third side.
  2. Use the Law of Cosines (or the Law of Sines) to find a second angle. Finding the smaller angle first avoids the ambiguous case.
  3. Find the last angle with α + β + γ = 180°.

03 · SSS: three sides

Procedure

First check the triangle inequality: each side must be shorter than the sum of the other two. Then find two angles with the inverse-cosine forms and the third from the angle sum. The largest angle is across from the longest side; finding it with cos⁻¹ also tells you whether the triangle is obtuse.

04 · Heron's formula

Area from three sides

Area = √(s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)), where s = ½(a + b + c) is the semi-perimeter.
