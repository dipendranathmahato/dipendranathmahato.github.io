Where we are coming from

In 7.5 Part II · Solving Trigonometric Equations: Factoring and Identities, factoring, the quadratic formula, and identities reduce harder equations to simple ones.

Right-triangle trigonometry needs a 90° angle. Most triangles, such as the one formed by two observers and a bird, or two tracking stations and a satellite, are oblique: no angle is 90°. The Law of Sines relates each side to the sine of the angle across from it, and solves an oblique triangle whenever we know an angle together with the side opposite it.

Learning objectives Use the Law of Sines to solve oblique triangles; find the area of an oblique triangle using the sine function; solve applied problems using the Law of Sines.

01 · The Law of Sines

Law of Sines

In a triangle with angles α, β, γ opposite sides a, b, c,

sin α / a = sin β / b = sin γ / c, or equivalently a / sin α = b / sin β = c / sin γ.

Solving a triangle means finding all three sides and all three angles. The Law of Sines works for ASA (two angles and the included side), AAS (two angles and a side opposite one of them), and SSA (two sides and an angle opposite one of them).

03 · SSA: the ambiguous case

How many triangles?

Given a, b, and α (opposite a), let h = b sin α be the height from C to the line through A. When α is acute:

  • a < h: no triangle (side a cannot reach the base).
  • a = h: one right triangle.
  • h < a < b: two triangles, with β₂ = 180° − β₁.
  • a ≥ b: one triangle.

When α is obtuse there is one triangle if a > b and none otherwise. With a calculator: if sin β = (b sin α)/a > 1 there is no triangle.

INTERACTIVE · SWING SIDE a

Side a swings around C like a compass. Every place it meets the base line is a possible vertex B.

04 · The area of an oblique triangle

Area from two sides and the included angle

Area = ½ ab sin γ = ½ bc sin α = ½ ac sin β

The factor b sin α is the height of the triangle, so this is ½ · base · height in disguise.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · Non-right Triangles: Law of Sines

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. Solve the triangle with β = 52°, γ = 96°, a = 15. Round to the nearest tenth.

    Show answer

    α = 180° − 52° − 96° = 32°. b = 15 sin 52°/sin 32° ≈ 22.3 and c = 15 sin 96°/sin 32° ≈ 28.2.

  2. How many triangles have α = 34°, a = 6, b = 9? Solve them.

    Show answer

    h = 9 sin 34° ≈ 5.03 < 6 < 9, so two. sin β = 9 sin 34°/6 ≈ 0.8388: β₁ ≈ 57.0°, γ₁ ≈ 89.0°, c₁ ≈ 10.7; β₂ ≈ 123.0°, γ₂ ≈ 23.0°, c₂ ≈ 4.2.

  3. Find the area of triangle ABC with γ = 62°, a = 28, b = 20.

    Show answer

    Area = ½(28)(20) sin 62° ≈ 247.2 square units.
