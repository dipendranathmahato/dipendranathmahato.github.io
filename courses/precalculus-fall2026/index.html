Chapter 5

Trigonometric Functions

4 sections · Weeks 1–3

SECTION 5.1Week 1AnglesStandard position, degrees and radians, coterminal and reference angles, arc length, sector area, and linear and angular speed. How do we measure a turn, and what happens after one full rotation?SECTION 5.2Week 2Unit Circle: Sine and Cosine FunctionsThe unit circle, sin t = y and cos t = x, the Pythagorean identity, special angles, and reference angles. How can a point moving around a circle define sine and cosine?SECTION 5.3Week 2The Other Trigonometric FunctionsTangent, cotangent, secant, and cosecant; even and odd functions; fundamental identities; calculator values. What other ratios can we build from the point (x, y)?SECTION 5.4Week 3Right Triangle TrigonometrySOH-CAH-TOA, missing sides, cofunctions of complementary angles, and angles of elevation and depression. How do the angles of a right triangle control the ratios of its sides?
Chapter 6

Periodic Functions

4 sections · Weeks 4–6

SECTION 6.1Week 4Graphs of the Sine and Cosine FunctionsPeriodic functions, amplitude, period, phase shift, vertical shift, and midline of y = A sin(Bx − C) + D. What do sine and cosine look like when we unroll the unit circle?SECTION 6.2 PARTS I–IIWeek 5Graphs of Tangent and CotangentPeriod π, vertical asymptotes, and reference points of y = A tan(Bx − C) + D and y = A cot(Bx − C) + D. What happens to a trig graph when the function divides by zero?SECTION 6.2 PART IIIWeek 5Graphs of Secant and CosecantSecant and cosecant from their cosine and sine guide curves: asymptotes, domain, range, and shifts. How does a reciprocal turn a smooth wave into U-shaped branches?SECTION 6.3Week 6Inverse Trigonometric FunctionsRestricted domains, arcsin, arccos, arctan, exact and calculator values, and compositions. If we know the value of sine, can we recover the angle?
Chapter 7

Trigonometric Identities and Equations

5 sections · Weeks 7–9

SECTION 7.1Week 7Solving Trigonometric Equations with IdentitiesPythagorean, reciprocal, quotient, and even-odd identities; verifying identities and simplifying expressions. How do we prove that two different-looking expressions are always equal?SECTION 7.2Week 7Sum and Difference IdentitiesSum and difference formulas for cosine, sine, and tangent, with exact values and given-quadrant problems. How can two special angles give exact values for angles like 15° and 105°?SECTION 7.3Week 8Double-Angle and Half-Angle FormulasDouble-angle formulas for sine, cosine, and tangent; half-angle formulas and choosing the sign. What happens to sine and cosine when an angle is doubled or halved?SECTION 7.5 PART IWeek 8Solving Trigonometric Equations: Linear EquationsLinear equations in one trig function, solutions in an interval and general solutions, and calculator angles. Which angles make a trig equation true, and how many are there?SECTION 7.5 PART IIWeek 9Solving Trigonometric Equations: Factoring and IdentitiesEquations in factored and quadratic form, rejecting impossible values, and using identities before solving. How do factoring and identities break a hard trig equation into easy pieces?
Chapter 8

Further Applications of Trigonometry

4 sections · Weeks 10–12

SECTION 8.1Week 10Non-right Triangles: Law of SinesThe Law of Sines for ASA, AAS, and the ambiguous SSA case; area with sine; applications. How do we solve a triangle that has no right angle?SECTION 8.2Week 10Non-right Triangles: Law of CosinesThe Law of Cosines for SAS and SSS triangles, navigation problems, and Heron's formula. What replaces the Pythagorean Theorem when the angle is not 90°?SECTION 8.8Week 11VectorsComponent form, magnitude and direction, vector arithmetic, unit vectors, i and j, and the dot product. How do we describe a quantity that has both size and direction?SECTION 8.3Week 12Polar CoordinatesPlotting polar points, converting points between polar and rectangular form, and converting equations. Can we locate a point by distance and angle instead of x and y?
Chapter 10

Analytic Geometry

4 sections · Weeks 12–14

SECTION 10.1 PART IWeek 12The Ellipse: Definition and EquationsFoci, vertices, and co-vertices; a² = b² + c²; writing standard equations of ellipses centered at the origin. What curve keeps the sum of two distances constant?SECTION 10.1 PART IIWeek 13The Ellipse: From Equation to GraphOrientation, vertices, co-vertices, foci, and axis lengths from the equation, and graphing ellipses. How do we read an ellipse's shape from its equation?SECTION 10.2Week 13The HyperbolaTransverse and conjugate axes, c² = a² + b², asymptotes and the central rectangle, writing and reading equations. What curve keeps the difference of two distances constant?SECTION 10.3Week 14The ParabolaFocus, directrix, and latus rectum; x² = 4py and y² = 4px; writing and reading equations. Which points are the same distance from a point and a line?

Learning objectives

Apply properties of functions and inverse functions; graph algebraic and transcendental functions; compute trigonometric values for key angles in all quadrants in degrees and radians; prove trigonometric identities; solve right and oblique triangles; convert between polar and rectangular coordinates; work with vectors geometrically and algebraically; and recognize the conic sections and their geometric properties.

Resources & academic integrity

Free textbook: OpenStax Precalculus 2e. A scientific calculator is recommended. Programmable calculators such as the NSpire are not allowed. Desmos and other online calculators may be used for assignments but not on tests.

Under the course AI policy, you may use generative AI tools when their use supports the learning goals of an assignment, as long as you remain the intellectual author and document how you used them. AI and other unauthorized resources are not allowed on exams, quizzes, or projects. These interactive notes provide independent, ungraded practice.
