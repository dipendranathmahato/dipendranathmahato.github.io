Trigonometry starts with a turn. An angle is made by rotating a ray about its endpoint, and the amount of rotation can be measured in degrees or in radians. In this section we draw angles in standard position, convert between the two units, find coterminal and reference angles, and use radians to measure arcs, sectors, and circular motion.

Learning objectives Draw angles in standard position; convert between degrees and radians; find coterminal angles; find the length of a circular arc and the area of a sector; use linear and angular speed to describe motion on a circular path.

01 · Angles in standard position

Definitions · Angle and standard position

An angle is the union of two rays with a common endpoint called the vertex. The ray where the rotation starts is the initial side, and the ray where it stops is the terminal side.

An angle is in standard position when its vertex is at the origin and its initial side lies along the positive x-axis. A counterclockwise rotation gives a positive angle, and a clockwise rotation gives a negative angle. A quadrantal angle has its terminal side on an axis: 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 360°, ….

02 · Degrees and radians

Two units for one rotation

One degree (1°) is 1/360 of a full rotation, so a full circle is 360°. One radian is the central angle whose arc has the same length as the radius. A full circle is 2π radians, so 180° = π radians and 1 radian = 180°/π ≈ 57.3°.

  • Degrees → radians: multiply by π/180°.
  • Radians → degrees: multiply by 180°/π.
INTERACTIVE · WHAT IS ONE RADIAN?

Lay copies of the radius along the circle. Each radius-length of arc adds one radian to the central angle.

INTERACTIVE · CONVERTER WITH STEPS

Type an angle such as 45, −55, or 530 in degrees, or 5π/6, −3π/2, or 2 in radians. Type π as π or pi.

COMMON MISTAKES
  • Simplify the fraction: 45° = 45π/180 = π/4, not 45π/180.
  • No degree sign on radians: write 5π/6 or 5π/6 rad, never 5π/6°.
  • “2” means 2 radians (about 114.6°), not 2°.

03 · Coterminal angles and reference angles

Definitions · Coterminal and reference angles

Two angles in standard position are coterminal if they have the same terminal side, for example 30° and 390°. To find the coterminal angle between 0° and 360° (or 0 and 2π), subtract 360° (2π) from an angle that is too large, or add 360° (2π) to a negative angle, as many times as needed.

The reference angle θ′ of an angle θ is the acute angle formed by the terminal side and the x-axis. For θ between 0° and 360°:

  • Quadrant I: θ′ = θ
  • Quadrant II or III: θ′ = |180° − θ| (or |π − θ|)
  • Quadrant IV: θ′ = 360° − θ (or 2π − θ)
  • Other angles: find the coterminal angle first, then its reference angle.

04 · Arc length and the area of a sector

Formulas · θ must be in radians

On a circle of radius r, a central angle θ measured in radians cuts off an arc and a sector with

arc length s = rθ and area A = ½ r²θ.

If θ is given in degrees, convert it to radians first.

05 · Linear and angular speed

Definitions · Motion on a circular path of radius r
  • Linear speed v is distance traveled (arc length s) per unit time: v = s/t.
  • Angular speed ω is the angle turned per unit time: ω = θ/t.
  • When ω is in radians per unit time, v = rω.
  • One rotation is 2π radians, so N rotations per unit time give ω = 2πN radians per unit time.

  1. Convert 40° and −55° to radians, and convert 11π/6 to degrees.

  2. Find the angle in [0°, 360°) coterminal with 861°, and the angle in [0, 2π) coterminal with −12π/7.

    Show answer
