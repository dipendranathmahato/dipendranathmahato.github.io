Where we are coming from

In 10.1 Part I · The Ellipse: Definition and Equations, an ellipse keeps the sum of the distances to its foci equal to 2a, with a² = b² + c².

In Part I we built the equation of an ellipse from its features. Now we go the other way: start from an equation such as x²/9 + y²/16 = 1, read off a, b, and c, list the vertices, co-vertices, and foci, and draw the graph.

Learning objectives Identify the major axis from an equation in standard form; find the vertices, co-vertices, foci, and axis lengths; graph ellipses centered at the origin.

01 · Reading the features from the equation

From x²/(denominator) + y²/(denominator) = 1
  1. The larger denominator is a²; the smaller is b². The variable over a² names the major axis.
  2. Find c from c² = a² − b².
  3. Major axis along x: vertices (±a, 0), co-vertices (0, ±b), foci (±c, 0). Along y: vertices (0, ±a), co-vertices (±b, 0), foci (0, ±c).
  4. The major axis has length 2a and the minor axis has length 2b.
INTERACTIVE · EQUATION → FEATURES

Presets are Example 2 and Checkpoint 2. Choose "Set your own" and type any two positive denominators (whole numbers or fractions).

NOTE ON EXAMPLE 2 (i)

For x²/18 + y²/15 = 1, c² = 18 − 15 = 3, so c = √3 (not 3) and the foci are (±√3, 0). The vertices (±3√2, 0), the co-vertices (0, ±√15), and the major-axis length 6√2 are as in the notes. Check: c must be smaller than a = 3√2 ≈ 4.24, and √3 ≈ 1.73 is.

02 · Graphing in four steps

To graph an ellipse centered at the origin
  1. Plot the center (0, 0).
  2. Plot the two vertices, a units from the center along the major axis.
  3. Plot the two co-vertices, b units from the center along the minor axis.
  4. Draw a smooth oval through the four points. Then mark the foci, c units from the center on the major axis.
INTERACTIVE · ONE STEP AT A TIME

Pick an equation and press the step buttons. Square roots are plotted at their decimal positions, for example √15 ≈ 3.87.

03 · Equations not yet in standard form

Divide to get 1 on the right

An equation like 4x² + 25y² = 100 is an ellipse, but it is not in standard form. Divide every term by the constant on the right: 4x²/100 + 25y²/100 = 1, which simplifies to x²/25 + y²/4 = 1. Then read the features as before.

INTERACTIVE · Ax² + By² = C

Use positive whole numbers A, B, and C.

WRAP-UP

Check your understanding · The Ellipse: From Equation to Graph

Answer each question on paper first, then open the answer to check yourself.

  1. For x²/30 + y²/20 = 1, find the vertices, co-vertices, foci, and the length of the major axis.

    Show answer

    a² = 30 and b² = 20, so a = √30, b = 2√5, and c² = 10, c = √10. Major axis along x: vertices (±√30, 0), co-vertices (0, ±2√5), foci (±√10, 0); length of the major axis 2√30.

  2. For x²/25 + y²/40 = 1, find the vertices, co-vertices, foci, and the length of the major axis.

    Show answer

    a² = 40 (under y²) and b² = 25, so a = 2√10, b = 5, and c² = 15, c = √15. Major axis along y: vertices (0, ±2√10), co-vertices (±5, 0), foci (0, ±√15); length of the major axis 4√10.

  3. Write 9x² + 4y² = 36 in standard form and name the vertices.

    Show answer

    Divide by 36: x²/4 + y²/9 = 1. Then a² = 9 is under y², so the vertices are (0, ±3).
